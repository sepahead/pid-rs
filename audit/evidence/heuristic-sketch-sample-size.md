# Why the continuous heuristic sketch grows with sample size

Sepehr Mahmoudian, 2026

Can a growing score under independence serve as evidence for shared information? The project-defined Rust function `heuristic_sketch_baseline` exposes one historical nearest-neighbor formula. Its smallest possible exact score grows without limit as the number of admitted rows grows at fixed neighbor rank. Two four-row examples below attain opposite endpoints. This gives a concrete reason to use the function only to study the formula. The defining continuous shared-exclusions functional and its estimator come from Ehrlich et al. Under mutually independent sources and target their population density ratio in Definition 2 equals one, giving zero shared information. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, Definition 2 and Eq. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06373v3) · [Original project formula](https://github.com/sepahead/pid-rs/blob/7c300aa2d5c09227e24f311f2b7fae8777467f18/crates/pid-core/src/isx.rs#L1449-L1557)

## The two target counts

Each of $n$ aligned rows contains two source vectors and one target vector. A neighbor is a different row. For each source separately, the joint distance to a neighbor is the larger of the source distance and the target distance. Each block uses the largest absolute coordinate difference. The neighbor rank $k$ is an integer from 1 to $n-1$. The radius for a source is its $k$th smallest joint distance. The code checks for exactly one neighbor on that boundary and exactly $k-1$ strictly inside it. The target counts $a_i,b_i$ for row $i$ count neighbors whose **target distance alone** is strictly below each of the two joint radii. All logarithms are natural and the score is in nats. Write $H_m=\sum_{j=1}^{m}1/j$, with $H_0=0$, and $\psi(m)=H_{m-1}-\gamma$ for the digamma function at a positive integer. Here $\gamma$ is Euler's constant. [NIST DLMF, integer digamma values](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.4.E14)

Every neighbor inside a joint radius is inside its target ball. Both target counts therefore lie between $k-1$ and $n-1$. The target count at the smaller radius is exactly $\min(a_i,b_i)$ because both radii apply to the same target distances. Subtracting the mean of the two digamma values from the smaller one yields half their absolute difference with a minus sign. Thus the ideal score $R$ obeys

$$
R=\psi(k)+\psi(n)-\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n |H_{a_i}-H_{b_i}|,\qquad
\frac{3\psi(k)+\psi(n)}{2}\le R\le \psi(k)+\psi(n).
$$

Both inequalities follow from $0\le |H_{a_i}-H_{b_i}|\le H_{n-1}-H_{k-1}$. At fixed $k$ the lower endpoint grows as $(\ln n)/2$ plus a bounded term. This conclusion holds for every growing sequence on which the exact count formula is defined. For independent scalar standard-normal inputs, ties have probability zero at each finite sample size in ideal independent sampling, and hence on all finite prefixes together. This includes an ordinary population where the shared information is zero. An exact coefficient of one for $\ln n$ at fixed $k$ would require the mean harmonic difference to be negligible relative to $\ln n$. A bounded remainder would require the mean difference to stay bounded in the corresponding mode of convergence. Neither follows from the finite count ranges alone. [NIST DLMF, asymptotic expansion](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11.E2)

## Finite endpoints and an independence comparison

Take scalar target readings $(0,1,4,10)$ and $k=2$. Make all first-source distances smaller than the smallest separation of the targets. The target then determines that joint radius and exactly one other target lies strictly inside each ball. If the second source is also compressed, both counts are one throughout. Instead set the second source to $(0,32,128,512)$. Each of its joint radii now exceeds the target span, giving three target neighbors at every row. All relevant shells are unique. The two resulting scores are $17/6-2\gamma$ and $29/12-2\gamma$, approximately 1.678902 and 1.262235 nats. The four-row Rust test gives the source coordinates. [Direct Rust regression](../../crates/pid-core/tests/isx.rs)

For any permitted finite $n,k$, take distinct target coordinates with unique distances from each row. Compress both sources for the upper endpoint. For the lower endpoint, compress the first source and give the second source distinct spacings greater than the target span with unique distances from each row. The lower construction gives $k-1$ and $n-1$ target neighbors at every row. Small perturbations preserve the strict comparisons in both constructions. Each finite configuration therefore has a neighborhood of positive probability under independent standard normals. This is an existence statement at each fixed $n$.

For example, under independent uniform scalar inputs and a fixed interior source–target query, a joint ball of small radius $r$ has probability $4r^2$. At radius $c/\sqrt n$ the count in that ball is binomial with mean approaching $4c^2$. Taking $c$ small and large brackets the fixed-$k$ joint radius with probability as close to one as desired. The corresponding target strips have probability $2c/\sqrt n$ and contain order $\sqrt n$ observations in probability. Hence either individual target count divided by $n$ tends to zero in probability, and its logarithm grows as one half of $\ln n$ plus a term bounded in probability. This typical-row calculation supplies no rate for the average difference of the two logarithms.

## Rust implementation and computational cost

The Cargo feature `experimental-heuristics` exposes the historical scalar. The call accepts equal positive source dimensions, a positive target dimension, finite coordinates, the Chebyshev metric, zero tie erosion and an explicit full-dimensional regular-density and finite-information declaration. It checks coordinate ties, unique positive shells and resource limits. Precise duplicate readings or shells can give a typed refusal. Finite input validation cannot establish a population model. The exact formula above uses mathematical digamma values. The published Rust uses an approximation, ordinary binary64 arithmetic and compensated summation. The measured errors below cover the declared inputs. [Support checks](../../crates/pid-core/src/support.rs) · [Count and distance code](../../crates/pid-core/src/nn.rs) · [Scalar numerical operations](../../crates/pid-core/src/stats.rs)

For source dimension $d$ and target dimension $q$, the two joint neighbor searches and three target counts take $O(n^2(d+q))$ work. Excluding caller-owned inputs, the fixed-block computation uses linear storage for row counts, radii, the digamma table and one row of distances, with additional coordinate diagnostics. It performs no fitting or resampling. This is an offline comparison. The feature-free stable API excludes the sketch. Configured raw PID2 and the experimental Python migration calls can combine it with mutual-information terms. When those terms stay bounded, a growing redundancy gives increasingly negative unique terms and positive synergy in exact algebra. An actual raw result must also pass its separately rounded reconstruction checks. Complete PID2 reports require the cited Ehrlich estimator. The local-minimum and unweighted inclusion–exclusion baselines use different formulas.
