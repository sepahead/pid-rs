Which null makes a permutation of one measured variable a valid comparison? `permutation_rows_pvalue*` reorders one matrix and leaves every other matrix fixed. The research `permutation_pid3*` wrappers apply the same action to one source. At revision `1479fc5e0ca15e5ef9c5521f8bc7aa36f2a98d20`, Rust documentation described exchangeability of rows or blocks and claimed exact uniformity over the seeded generator. This record corrects those statements. [Rust implementation](../../crates/pid-core/src/pipeline.rs).

## Null, statistic and reference sampling law

Write $X$ for the full aligned data, $T$ for the predeclared scalar statistic and $G$ for the finite group of allowed transformations. Each $g$ in $G$ permutes only the selected matrix, with complete within-block order preserved for fixed block shuffles. Hemerik and Goeman's Definition 1 requires the joint distribution of $(T(gX))_{g\in G}$ to be unchanged when $X$ is replaced by $hX$ for each $h\in G$. Invariance of the full data law under each such $h$ suffices. Independent and identically distributed pairs $(U_i,U_i)$ illustrate the difference. Simultaneously reordering the pairs preserves their law. Reordering only the first component can destroy the exact equality within pairs. Dependence between blocks matters through this same symmetry condition. [Hemerik and Goeman, Definition 1 and Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11749-017-0571-1).

For the with-replacement reference test, include the observed identity transformation and draw $B$ independent uniform group elements independently of the data. If $b$ sampled values are at least the observed value, the upper-tail result is $(b+1)/(B+1)$. The lower tail counts values at most the observation. Counting ties in the chosen tail gives rejection probability at most the nominal level under the stated premises. Discreteness and ties can make the probability smaller. The null concerns alignment of the selected variable with the others. Each atom value is a statistic of that comparison. A zero-atom hypothesis requires its own justification. [Hemerik and Goeman, Sections 3.2–3.4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11749-017-0571-1), [Phipson and Smyth](https://doi.org/10.2202/1544-6115.1585).

## Execution and disposition

A seed fixes the entire SplitMix64 schedule. Rejection sampling removes modulo bias if its successive input words are independent and uniform. A uniform 64-bit seed has $2^{64}$ equally likely values. Exactly uniform selection of one of six permutations from a deterministic function of that seed would require an equal integer number of seeds per permutation, and six does not divide $2^{64}$. A seeded replay therefore identifies the transform sequence, while the theorem models ideal draws. This argument alone gives no numerical bound on a particular test's type I error. Every requested transform must yield a finite result for the report to provide a tail fraction. The admissible restricted circular shifts omit the identity and do not form a group. Their tail fractions remain stationary-surrogate scores.

The correction changes the interpretation of the existing computation. Source inspection checked the transformed matrix, Fisher–Yates draws, permitted block sizes and shifts, signed non-strict tail comparisons, and complete-outcome requirement. The existing regression suite checks seeded schedules, tails, failures and null declarations. These checks give no empirical or formal calibration claim. Reopen if the group action, offset set, random source, data-dependent selection, tail convention, or handling of failed transformations changes. An exact-uniformity or calibration claim would need evidence for its own randomization and data law. [Existing tests](../../crates/pid-core/tests/permutation_and_fdr.rs).
