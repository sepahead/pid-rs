# Relative truncation error for the averaged angular top atom

Sepehr Mahmoudian, 5 October 2026.

This is a project-derived, handwritten result for one specified continuous
population law. It establishes a positive denominator for the existing
two-harmonic approximation. For `0 < |a|+|b| <= 1/2`, retaining eight even
moments gives relative error at most `1/1253376 < 10^-6` in the averaged top
atom. Formal verification, a certified evaluator and sampling guarantees
remain open. No scientific-priority claim is made.

Suggested citation: Sepehr Mahmoudian, *Relative truncation error for the
averaged angular top atom* (2026), including the exact repository commit or
release when published. Cite the defining method paper separately.

The paper-defined functional is Ehrlich et al., *Partial Information
Decomposition for Continuous Variables based on Shared Exclusions:
Analytical Formulation and Estimation*,
[arXiv:2311.06373v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.06373v3), separately published
at [DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.110.014115](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.110.014115).
Sections II.2 and III.2, Equation (16), and Appendix B Definition 3 provide
the continuous branch sums, averaging and finite inversion. The current
audit inspected the versioned HTML. An exact local paper-byte binding is
not supplied by this packet. The model and bounds below are local
derivations, not theorems attributed to that paper.

Let `L=2*pi`, and let `S1,S2,S3,T` be scalar angles in `[0,L)`, in radians.
Fix product Lebesgue measure, real `a,b`, and `r=|a|+|b|<1`. Define

\[
 v=(t-s_1)\bmod L,\qquad u=(t-s_1-s_2-s_3)\bmod L,
 \qquad p(s_1,s_2,s_3,t)=L^{-4}(1+a\cos v+b\cos u).
\]

The density lies between `(1-r)L^-4` and `(1+r)L^-4`. Integrating over `t`
removes both cosines, giving `p_S=L^-3`; integrating over `s1` gives
`p_T=L^-1`. Thus the law is normalized, the three sources are independent
uniform angles, and the target is uniform. The torus density is positive
and smooth; seams have probability zero. The ratio to `p_S*p_T` is bounded
away from zero and infinity, so the information integrals below are finite.

This is a specified population model, not an empirical count law or a fit
to recordings. The identities need no IID sampling premise. A sampler,
estimator or recorded time series needs its own sampling assumptions.
Natural logarithms give nats. The scalar numerical densities use the fixed
radian gauge, including at mixed-cardinality nodes; replacing these with
unit-Haar marginal densities changes the defining branch sums. Uniform
probability measures used to calculate integrals below do not make that
replacement.

For a proper nonempty source subset `B`, integrating an omitted source
removes the `b` term. The target/source marginal is

\[
 p_{T,S_B}=L^{-|B|-1}
 \begin{cases}1+a\cos v,&1\in B,\\1,&1\notin B.\end{cases}
 \qquad p_{S_B}=L^{-|B|}.
\]

For a proper antichain `alpha`, the branch-sum log ratio is therefore
`ln(1+a*w_alpha*cos v)`, where

\[
 w_\alpha=
 \frac{\sum_{B\in\alpha,\,1\in B}L^{-|B|}}
      {\sum_{B\in\alpha}L^{-|B|}}.
\]

Under the actual law, `v` has density `(1+a*cos v)/L`. Define its averaged
cumulative for `0<=w<=1` by

\[
 R(w)=\frac1L\int_0^L(1+a\cos v)\ln(1+aw\cos v)\,dv.
 \qquad P=R(1/2),\quad D=R(2/3),\quad Q=R(1).
\]

The first `a` is the population weight; it cannot be replaced by `aw`.
The available exact elementary expression is

\[
 H(a,c)=\ln\frac{1+\sqrt{1-c^2}}2+
          \frac{ac}{1+\sqrt{1-c^2}},\qquad R(w)=H(a,aw).
\]

For completeness, put `z=c/(1+sqrt(1-c^2))` and
`K=(1+sqrt(1-c^2))/2`. Then `1+c*cos v=K*|1+z*exp(iv)|^2` and `|z|<1`.
Expand the logarithm in its uniformly absolutely convergent Fourier
series. Its uniform mean is `ln K` and its cosine-weighted mean is `z`.
This proves the displayed formula. The proof of the lower bound below
also works directly from the integral and requires no square-root or
logarithm evaluation.

## 2. Why the averaged top atom is J-Q-D+2P

For antichains use `beta<=alpha` when every branch of `alpha` contains a
branch of `beta`. Define atoms by the finite cumulative equations
`R_alpha=sum_{beta<=alpha} Pi_beta`. There are 18 positions for three
sources. The top is the one-branch antichain `{123}`; `A` denotes its
averaged atom, not its pointwise value.

Every proper antichain belongs to at least one of the lower sets of
`{12}`, `{13}`, `{23}`: select one proper branch and extend it to a pair.
The intersection of two such lower sets is the lower set of the antichain
containing the two pairs. The same statement holds for the triple
intersection. Finite inclusion-exclusion applies to the signed atom sum,
so the sum of all 17 proper atoms is

\[
 R_{\{12\}}+R_{\{13\}}+R_{\{23\}}
 -R_{\{12\}\{13\}}-R_{\{12\}\{23\}}-R_{\{13\}\{23\}}
 +R_{\{12\}\{13\}\{23\}}.
\]

All branches in these seven cumulatives have size two. Their weights are
respectively `1,1,0,1,1/2,1/2,2/3`, so this sum is `Q+D-2P`. This direct
top-cut argument independently reconstructs the predecessor's full-table
top coefficient without using the values of its mixed-cardinality atoms.

Let `h=L^-4` be the uniform integration reference. The coordinate change
`(s1,s2,s3,t) -> (s1,s2,v,u)` has the integer inverse
`t=v+s1`, `s3=v-u-s2` modulo `L`, with determinant of magnitude one.
Consequently `v,u` are independent uniform angles under `h`. Put
`Y=a*cos v+b*cos u`. The full mutual information is

\[
 J(a,b)=\mathbb E_h[(1+Y)\ln(1+Y)],\qquad Q=J(a,0).
\]

Since the top cumulative is `J`, the atom of interest is exactly

\[
 \boxed{A(a,b)=J(a,b)-Q-D+2P.}
\]

## 3. The existing positive series and absolute tail

For `|Y|<=r<1`, absolute uniform convergence permits integration of

\[
 (1+Y)\ln(1+Y)=Y+\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{(-1)^nY^n}{n(n-1)}.
\]

Shifting both angles by `pi` negates `Y` and preserves uniform measure,
so odd moments vanish. The even cosine mean is
`E cos^(2j)(v)=binom(2j,j)/4^j`: expand the power of
`(exp(iv)+exp(-iv))/2` and keep its constant Fourier term. Independence
then gives

\[
 J=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{M_m}{2m(2m-1)},\qquad
 M_m=\frac{(2m)!}{4^m}
 \sum_{j=0}^m\frac{a^{2j}b^{2m-2j}}{(j!)^2((m-j)!)^2}.
\]

All coefficients are nonnegative, including for negative `a` or `b`.
For integer `M>=1`, define `J_M` by retaining `m=1,...,M`. Since
`0<=M_m<=r^(2m)`,

\[
 0\le J-J_M\le B_M:=
 \frac{r^{2M+2}}{(2M+2)(2M+1)(1-r^2)}.
\]

Indeed the first omitted denominator is the smallest tail denominator;
replacing all subsequent ones by it leaves a geometric series. Define
`A_M=J_M-Q-D+2P`, with the proper terms kept exact. Then
`0<=A-A_M=J-J_M<=B_M`. A numerical implementation that approximates the
proper terms must add those errors separately.

## 4. A positive denominator for the atom

### 4.1 The additional harmonic contributes at least b²/4

In the convergent series for `J(a,b)-J(a,0)`, the `j=m` term cancels at
each `m`. Every remaining term is nonnegative. The `m=1,j=0` term is
`b²/4`. Therefore

\[
 J-Q\ge b^2/4.
\]

A second hand derivation uses curvature rather than the series. Write
`C=cos v`, `E=cos u` under the independent uniform reference. For fixed
`a` and any `t` on the segment from zero to `b`, differentiation under the
integral is justified by `1+aC+tE>=1-r>0`. Thus

\[
 \partial_b^2J(a,t)=\mathbb E\frac{E^2}{1+aC+tE}
 =\mathbb E\frac{E^2}{1-(aC+tE)^2}\ge\mathbb E E^2=1/2.
\]

The equality averages the integrand with its image under
`(C,E)->(-C,-E)`. Also `partial_b J(a,0)=0`, since `E` has zero mean and
is independent of `C`. The integral form of Taylor's theorem gives

\[
 J(a,b)-J(a,0)=b^2\int_0^1(1-s)\partial_b^2J(a,sb)\,ds\ge b^2/4.
\]

This argument includes both signs of `b`. It shares the stated law and
analytic differentiation premises with the first route; it is not an
independent institution or formal checker.

### 4.2 The proper terms contribute at least a²/8

Let `C=cos v` under the uniform reference. The denominator of the
integrand defining `R` is at least `1-|a|>0` for `0<=w<=1`, permitting
two derivatives under the bounded integral. They are

\[
 R'(w)=a^2(1-w)\mathbb E\frac{C^2}{1+awC},\qquad
 R''(w)=-a^2\mathbb E\frac{(1+aC)C^2}{(1+awC)^2}\le0.
\]

The first identity follows by writing
`(1+aC)/(1+awC)=1+a*(1-w)*C/(1+awC)` and using `E C=0`.
The second shows concavity, because `1+aC>0`. With `R(0)=0` and
`1/2=(3/4)*(2/3)+(1/4)*0`, concavity gives

\[
 P\ge\tfrac34D,\qquad 2P-D\ge\tfrac23P.
\]

Symmetry of `C` also gives

\[
 \mathbb E\frac{C^2}{1+awC}
 =\mathbb E\frac{C^2}{1-a^2w^2C^2}\ge\mathbb E C^2=1/2.
\]

Hence `R'(w)>=a²(1-w)/2`. Integrating from zero to `1/2` yields

\[
 P\ge\frac{a^2}{2}\left(\frac12-\frac18\right)
   =\frac{3a^2}{16},\qquad
 \boxed{2P-D\ge a^2/8.}
\]

The predecessor's closed-form derivative provides a check:
`R'(w)=a²(1-w)/(s*(1+s))`, where `s=sqrt(1-a²w²)`.
Since `0<s<=1`, its denominator is at most two, giving the same bound.
No division by `a`, `b`, `P` or `D` was used, so their zero cases survive.

### 4.3 Combine the two bounds

For every real `a,b` with `r<1`,

\[
 \boxed{A\ge a^2/8+b^2/4\ge r^2/12.}
\]

The final step is the exact square identity

\[
 \frac{a^2}{8}+\frac{b^2}{4}-\frac{(|a|+|b|)^2}{12}
 =\frac{(|a|-2|b|)^2}{24}\ge0.
\]

Thus `A>0` at every nonzero parameter pair in this domain. At the origin,
all cumulatives and atoms are zero; relative error is the undefined
quotient `0/0`. The bound is for the averaged top atom only.

## 5. Uniform relative error and worked exact values

For every `0<r<1` and integer `M>=1`, define relative truncation error by
`e_M=|A-A_M|/A`. The established sign of the tail and denominator imply

\[
 0\le e_M\le
 \frac{B_M}{a^2/8+b^2/4}
 \le\frac{12r^{2M}}{(2M+2)(2M+1)(1-r^2)}.
\]

Both `r^(2M)` and `1/(1-r²)` increase for `0<r<1`. At `M=8` and
`r<=1/2` the last expression is at most

\[
 \frac{12(1/2)^{16}}{18\cdot17\cdot(3/4)}
 =\frac1{1253376}<\frac1{1000000}.
\]

The last inequality is an integer comparison. In this domain `A_8>0`
also follows because the relative error ceiling is smaller than one.
The displayed bound tends to zero as `r` tends to zero, but this does not
define a relative error at the origin.

For the original example `a=b=1/4`,

\[
 r=1/2,\quad A\ge\frac{3}{128},\quad
 B_8=\frac1{60162048},\quad
 e_8\le\frac{128}{3\cdot60162048}=\frac1{1410048}.
\]

Here `J_1=1/32`, `J_2=131/4096`, and

\[
 J_8=\frac{3787665970322303}{118219490218475520},\qquad
 A_8=J_8-H(1/4,1/4)-H(1/4,1/6)+2H(1/4,1/8).
\]

Thus `[A_8,A_8+1/60162048]` is an exact-real enclosure. The endpoint is
a rational-plus-elementary expression; printing that expression does not
make it a machine-certified interval. The first moment formula gives
`J_1=(a²+b²)/4`; the next gives
`J_2=J_1+(a^4+b^4)/32+a²b²/8`, explaining both small fractions above.

All four examples below have `r=1/2` and the same `B_8=1/60162048`.

| a | b | Proven lower bound on A | Proven relative ceiling |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1/2 | 0 | 1/32 | 1/1880064 |
| 0 | 1/2 | 1/16 | 1/3760128 |
| 1/4 | 1/4 | 3/128 | 1/1410048 |
| 1/3 | 1/6 | 1/48 | 1/1253376 |

The last row attains equality only in the weighted-square lower-envelope
step. It is not asserted to attain the actual atom bound or tail error.
Changing either amplitude sign leaves the series and bounds unchanged.
For `M=7`, the same uniform argument gives `1/245760>10^-6`; this means
that this ceiling does not certify the target accuracy at order seven.
It does not prove that the actual order-seven error exceeds that target.

## 6. Counterexamples, failed routes and interpretation

*Pointwise positivity does not follow.* Set `a=0`, `b=1/4`, and `u=pi`.
Every proper pointwise cumulative is zero, but the pointwise top atom is
`ln(3/4)<0`. Its averaged top atom remains positive. Signed atoms are never
clamped by this result.

*The top atom is not necessarily added task information.* Set `b=0` and
`0<|a|<1`. Then `p(t|s1,s2,s3)=p(t|s1)`, so
`I(S2,S3;T|S1)=J-Q=0`. Nonetheless `A=2P-D>=a²/8>0`.
This model is useful to test the named decomposition and its interpretation.
MI/CMI suffices when the question is the additional information of the two
extra sources. Neither a positive top atom nor the small series error shows
predictive, sensor-selection or control benefit.

*The joint-MI denominator cannot be reused without proof.* For `b=0`,
`R'(w)>0` at `a!=0,w<1`, hence `Q>D>P>0` and `A=2P-D<Q=J`.
A bound with denominator `J` is not automatically a bound with denominator
`A`. The new lower-bound proof is the missing step.

*A tempting coefficientwise strengthening fails.* Expansion gives

\[
 R(w)=\sum_{m\ge1}a^{2m}\frac{\binom{2m}{m}}{4^m}
 \left(\frac{w^{2m-1}}{2m-1}-\frac{w^{2m}}{2m}\right).
\]

The leading coefficient of `2P-D` is `11/72`, but its `a^6` coefficient
is `-3089/2239488`. Consequently its higher coefficients are not all
nonnegative. Retaining only the leading coefficient cannot be justified
by the positive-series argument used for `J-Q`. This rejects that proof
route; by itself it neither proves nor refutes the stronger global
inequality `2P-D>=11a²/72`.

The present proof excludes `r=1`, where its uniform positive-denominator
argument and stated geometric tail cease to apply. For `r>1`, suitable
phases make the proposed density negative. Changed gauges, nonuniform or
dependent source laws, fitted amplitudes, rounded observations, singular
embeddings, other PID definitions and population estimation require new
premise-explicit arguments. The predecessor's mixed-dimensional raw-radius
contrast also remains: this analytic result does not repair that estimator.

## Rust implementation and computational cost

The exact estimand is the specified population averaged top atom `A(a,b)`.
This packet adds no public Rust implementation, feature or estimator. The
existing route is
`experimental::mixed_dimension_pid3::pid3_isx_report` (and the scalar
`pid3_isx`) in `crates/pid-core/src/pid3.rs`, re-exported by `src/lib.rs`
under `research-mixed-dimension-pid3`. It also requires the explicit runtime
mixed-dimension opt-in. It is a sample-based research route, not this
parameter-specified analytic reference. The incomplete PID3 API is an
availability diagnostic and cannot supply the missing full atoms.

The relevant source-derived baseline is the integration's
`RUST_IMPLEMENTATION_AND_COST.md`, originally anchored to
`8a1142cae32fae637b3bdfe39f890cb29176d144`; current exports and the warning
beside `pid3_isx` were also read. The existing PID3 implementation retains
four triangular distance matrices, with distance payload `16*N*(N-1)`
bytes before scratch and other allocations. Its resource preflight and
research warnings remain applicable. No timing is reported here.

For known parameters, the analytic calculation has no data rows, training
set, empirical alphabet or occupied support. Its population support is
the full four-dimensional angle box (with periodic seams). A possible
sampled diagnostic would take `N` aligned rows of four scalar angles,
three source columns and one target column. Fitting parameters or
resampling rows would be additional work and additional error sources;
neither is implemented or covered by this theorem.

Computing the first `M` exact moments visits `M*(M+3)/2` nonnegative summands,
44 at `M=8`. Rational parameter powers and factorial/binomial coefficients
can be reused. A streaming evaluator need retain only its current
coefficient, powers, moment and accumulated sum, or `O(M)` rational
objects if powers/factorials are cached. These counts are not constant-bit
time or memory bounds: numerator and denominator bit lengths and GCD
costs grow. Fixed-width factorial overflow, saturation and modular
arithmetic cannot silently replace exact rational arithmetic.

The small accompanying Python script checks finite rational formulas and
the 18-node top cut. It does not evaluate `H`, produce an outward interval
for `A`, or constitute a production evaluator. An actual Rust interval
route needs exact or outward input bounds, qualified square-root/logarithm
enclosures, and propagation of every proper-term error through
`J_M-Q-D+2P`. `B_M` covers only the omitted `J` terms. Near zero, a lower
denominator on the scale `r²` makes uncaptured absolute rounding errors
especially relevant. No cancellation, memory, deadline or failure contract
for that proposed evaluator is implemented here.

The intended use is an offline analytic reference and interpretation test.
A supplied-parameter ceiling costs few scalar operations; obtaining valid
model parameters and guarantees for recorded data is a separate problem.
There is no online or real-time qualification. Formal proof checking is
separate from both formula evaluation and estimator runtime and is UNRUN.
