## Object and assumptions

The categorical object is the finite-law shared-exclusions measure of
[Makkeh, Gutknecht and Wibral, version 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03356v5).
For source indices $1,\ldots,n$, an antichain $\alpha$ is a nonempty family of nonempty
source groups, with no group containing another. At a supported observation
$z=(s_1,\ldots,s_n,t)$, define

$$
A_\alpha(z)=\bigcup_{a\in\alpha}\bigcap_{i\in a}\{S_i=s_i\}.
$$

Thus a group uses AND; the family uses OR. Write $u=P(A_\alpha)$,
$j=P(A_\alpha,T=t)$ and $q=P(T=t)$. Support gives $0<j\le\min(u,q)$.
The cumulative components, in nats, are

$$
c^+=-\log u,\qquad c^-=\log(q/j),\qquad
c^{\rm net}=\log\frac{j}{uq}.
$$

For $\beta\preceq\alpha$ when each group in $\alpha$ contains a group in
$\beta$, each component's pointwise atom vector $\pi(z)$ solves
$c_\alpha(z)=\sum_{\beta\preceq\alpha}\pi_\beta(z)$.
Finite Möbius inversion solves this linear system. For $p(z)=P(Z=z)$, define
averaged net atoms $\Pi_\alpha=\sum_{z:p(z)>0}p(z)\pi_\alpha^{\rm net}(z)$.
Informative and misinformative component nonnegativity does not imply net
nonnegativity. Negative net atoms must remain visible.

Categorical MGW, continuous Ehrlich, Williams--Beer $I_{\min}$ and KSG are
different objects. Quantization, projection and added noise change variables
or laws. A support declaration is an assumption, not a property inferred from
unique observed rows. The continuous full PID3 path remains research-only.

The finite reference calculations use explicitly specified laws and exact rational
arithmetic or exact rational log-products. Floating replicas are labeled separately.
Where rows are called IID, complete
source-and-target rows are independent and identically distributed; coordinates
within a row need not be independent. Files are assumed stable during bounded
reads. Hash equality is not authenticity, atomicity or independent custody.
Python mode repeats share code. Model critiques are advisory, not independent
human review. Decimal illustrations are not directed logarithm enclosures.

### A nonempty expression can be exactly zero

In binary cell order $(S_1,S_2,T)$, use counts
$(0,0,1,1,1,4,1,0)$, total eight. For each supported anchor, compute its target
count, singleton count and union count, form the two net cumulatives, subtract
the union cumulative from the first singleton cumulative, and average with
count/eight weights. For counts $N,U,J,Q$ (total, event, event-target intersection
and target), the log argument is $NJ/(UQ)$. At anchor 010, the singleton has
$U=2,J=1,Q=3$ and gives $8/(2\cdot3)=4/3$; the union has $U=3,J=2,Q=3$
and gives $16/9$. The full supported-row calculation is:

| Anchor | Count | First singleton ratio | Union ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 010 | 1 | 4/3 | 16/9 |
| 011 | 1 | 4/5 | 8/15 |
| 100 | 1 | 8/9 | 8/9 |
| 101 | 4 | 16/15 | 16/15 |
| 110 | 1 | 8/9 | 1 |

The 100 and 101 rows cancel within their singleton-minus-union differences.
The other rows give five nonzero log terms. Clearing the denominator yields

$$
8V=\log(4/5)+\log(8/9)+\log(4/3)

$$

The numerator product is $128/135$; the denominator product is also $128/135$.
Hence $8V=\log 1=0$, so $V=0$. A nonempty canonical term list cannot prove a
nonzero value. This reconstructs a retained completeness counterexample;

## Prefix means and score derivatives

For the one-source block, let $Z_0=(S_0,T_0)$ be the anchor and
$Z_1,\ldots,Z_h$ the later rows. Define $D_k=\mathbf1\{S_k\ne S_0\}$ and
$R_j=\sum_{k=1}^j\mathbf1\{T_k=T_0\}$, the number of retained target matches.
The native block is

$$
B_h=\sum_{j=1}^h\left[
 \frac{\prod_{k=1}^jD_k}{j}
 -\mathbf1\{T_j=T_0\}
  \frac{\prod_{1\le k\le j:\,T_k=T_0}D_k}{R_j}\right].
$$

The second term is defined as zero when $R_j=0$; an empty product is one.
It contributes only on a current target-match arrival. Thus
$B_1=\mathbf1\{S_1\ne S_0,\ T_1\ne T_0\}$.
Raw time $j$ and retained rank $R_j$ are different. Direct enumeration and a
separate event-mass polynomial agree for three two-source laws, horizons zero
through three, and all four atoms: 14,040 complete words, including zero-mass
words. Only one law tests a nonzero tangent.

For one independent fair source and target at horizon two, each anchor gives
the same calculation. The positive mean is
$1/2+(1/2)^2/2=5/8$. Negative contributions are first arrival $1/4$, a target
miss followed by a target match and source miss $1/8$, and two retained misses
$1/16$ divided by rank two. Thus

$$
E B_2=\frac58-\left(\frac14+\frac18+\frac1{32}\right)=\frac7{32}.
$$

Replacing $R_2=1$ by raw rank two halves the $1/8$ target-miss/arrival
subtraction, giving $7/32+1/16=9/32$. Recounting an earlier exclusion when
the second row is not an arrival adds a $1/8$ subtraction, giving
$7/32-1/8=3/32$. Both incorrect rules are retained as causal controls.

For a differentiable normalized positive finite law $p_\theta$ and a parameter-independent
block $B$ on $h+1$ IID rows, write $s_\theta(z)=p'_\theta(z)/p_\theta(z)$.
Differentiating the finite sum, then each product, gives

$$
\frac{d}{d\theta}E_\theta B
=\sum_w B(w)\sum_{r=0}^{h}p'_\theta(w_r)
                   \prod_{k\ne r}p_\theta(w_k)
=E_\theta\left[B\sum_{r=0}^{h}s_\theta(Z_r)\right].
$$

The score sum includes the random anchor $r=0$. At one source, horizon one,
use diagonal masses $\theta/2$ and off-diagonal masses $(1-\theta)/2$.
The block is one exactly when both bits differ between the two rows. Its mean
is $(\theta^2+(1-\theta)^2)/2$, whose derivative is $2\theta-1$.
At $\theta=3/4$ the full derivative is $1/2$; omitting the anchor score gives
$1/4$ by exchangeability of the two IID rows.

### Equal marginals do not justify an IID second moment

At $\theta=3/4$, the scores in binary order are $(4/3,-4,-4,4/3)$ and
$E s^2=16/3$. Instead of IID rows, take
$Z_1=(1-S_0,1-T_0)$. Complement preserves the displayed law, so both marginals
remain correct. Both bits differ, hence $B_1=1$; the two scores are equal.
Consequently

$$
E\left[\bigl(B_1(s(Z_0)+s(Z_1))\bigr)^2\right]=4E s^2=64/3.
$$

For IID rows, normalization gives $E s=\sum_z p'_\theta(z)=0$.
Since $|B_1|\le1$, independence gives
$E[B_1^2(s_0+s_1)^2]\le E[(s_0+s_1)^2]=2E s^2$.
This IID unit-range upper bound is $32/3$ and is exceeded. It is not the
actual IID block-score moment: that value is
$2(3/8)^2(8/3)^2+2(1/8)^2(8)^2=4$. The weaker envelope 32 is not exceeded.
Identical rows give block zero and fail as an
attack; that failed construction remains archived. The successor diagnostic
constructs the actual complement pairs, checks both marginals and evaluates
the native block. This is a finite counterexample to dropping IID, not a
confidence-coverage or optimization theorem.

Complete-key return cancels later native increments; source-only return does
not. The latter can leave a contribution of $-1$. The bounded deterministic
return check covers 512 words. No infinite stopping or terminal-score deletion
theorem is inferred. Decimal cusp examples illustrate a handwritten boundary:
support-changing differentiation remains separate from common-support scores.

## Sensor availability without an extra PID objective

Assume a finite sensor/target law, an exogenous availability mask $M$ jointly
independent of all sensors and target, and an oracle using true conditional
probabilities with natural-log loss. The baseline receives the full mask but no
sensor readings; the augmented oracle receives that same mask and the available
readings. Their optimal losses are $H(T\mid M)$ and $H(T\mid M,S_M)$, so their
gain is $I(T;S_M\mid M)$. Joint mask independence reduces each conditional law
to the specified sensor/target law. For $q(m)=P(M=m)$, the gain is therefore

$$
J=\sum_m q(m)I(S_m;T).
$$

The empty subset contributes zero. Self-redundancy and inversion give
$I(S_m;T)=\sum_{\alpha\preceq\{m\}}\Pi_\alpha$ for nonempty $m$.
The condition $\alpha\preceq\{m\}$ means that some branch $a\in\alpha$
is contained in $m$. Interchanging two finite sums therefore gives

$$
J=\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha w_\alpha,\qquad
w_\alpha=\sum_m q(m)\mathbf1\{\exists a\in\alpha:a\subseteq m\}.
$$

Thus this oracle objective is fixed by subset MI; rewriting it in PID coordinates
does not supply an extra optimization criterion. Overlapping branches must be
counted once. For two independent fair availability bits and singleton branches
$\{1\},\{2\}$, three of four masks qualify: $w=3/4$, not $1/2+1/2=1$.
Fresh exact checks cover nine laws at two, three and four sources, 75 nonempty
subset identities and 18 weighted objectives. They do not prove sampled utility.

### Equal synergy can mean different added value

On the same eight equally likely worlds, let $A,B,U$ be independent fair bits,
target $T=(A,B)$ and base sensor $A$. Compare added sensors $U$, $B$ and $A$.
For $U$ and $B$, the source union has probability $3/4$ and its target-restricted
probability is $1/4$. Redundancy is $\log(4/3)$. Using
$\mathrm{Syn}=I(A,C;T)-I(A;T)-I(C;T)+\mathrm{Red}$ gives synergy
$\log(4/3)$ in both cases. A duplicate has synergy zero.

Let base and added availability be independent with probabilities $4/5$ and
$9/10$, also jointly independent of the bits. Both compared oracles see the same
full mask; the baseline gets available base readings, and the augmented oracle
also gets available added readings. Added noise has gain zero.
The complementary bit is useful whenever it arrives, so its gain is
$(9/10)\log 2$. A duplicate is useful only when it arrives and the base fails,
so its gain is $(9/10)(1/5)\log 2=(9/50)\log 2$.
Equal synergy therefore does not identify this task's gain.

Pairwise mask independence is insufficient. For independent fair $X,Y$, set
$M=1$ when $X=Y$. Then $M$ is independent of each individual bit, but not of
their joint state. Conditional on observed $M$, seeing $X$ determines $Y$.
If $X$ is shown only when $M=1$, its gain given the mask is $(1/2)\log 2$,
despite $I(X;Y)=0$. The exogenous-mask formula cannot be used under this selection.

## A finite fault result and real application limits

For the retained binary fault game, set target $T=S_1$, diagonal masses $9/20$
and off-diagonal masses $1/20$. A report must preserve at least one coordinate.
Forecast probabilities are strictly positive and sum to one. Worst report loss
is averaged under this fixed law.

The admissible source-only attack sends 00 to 01 and 10 with probabilities
$4/9,5/9$, sends 11 with the opposite probabilities, and leaves 01 and 10
unchanged. Each of the four target/report cells has mass $1/4$:
$(9/20)(4/9)+1/20=1/4$ and $(9/20)(5/9)=1/4$.
Each surviving report therefore has a fair target.

For positive forecast masses $a+b=1$, $(a-b)^2\ge0$ gives $ab\le1/4$.
Taking logs gives $(-\log a-\log b)/2\ge\log 2$. Worst loss dominates the
attack's mean, so every forecast has risk at least $\log 2$. Constant fair
forecasting attains it. This establishes the finite game's value.
The event-consistency forecast is
$f_C(t\mid r)=P(T=t\mid S_1=r_1\ \text{or}\ S_2=r_2)$.
For reports 00, 01, 10 and 11, its target-zero probabilities are respectively
$10/11,10/19,9/19,1/11$. Diagonal states have worst correct-target probability
$9/19$; off-diagonal states have $1/11$. Their total masses are $9/10$ and
$1/10$, so its worst risk is

$$
\frac9{10}\log(19/9)+\frac1{10}\log 11>\log 2,
$$

because both log arguments exceed two. Its risk is about 0.91228249 nats.
This refutes the proposed minimax extension, not the published MGW measure.
