# A cancellation-free relative enclosure for signed empirical averages

Sepehr Mahmoudian · 11 October 2026

## Question, result and status

Can a very small nonzero signed categorical shared-exclusions average be
evaluated with a guaranteed relative error, rather than subtracting large
nearly equal components?

Under exact integer count weights and the MGW component/inverse identity,
the answer is yes in exact rational arithmetic. First combine all terms
into one rational ratio. Compare its integer numerator and denominator to
decide sign or exact zero. Then evaluate the magnitude by a positive
logarithm series. Five raw rational terms have a uniform midpoint relative
ceiling of \(1/1154736\), below one part per million. This does not need a
minimum nonzero magnitude or a count-cap separation gap.

Two independent-first hand derivations agree; root has read the complete
subsequent source critique. This is not an accepted formal theorem,
implemented certificate, timed result, human verification or scientific-
priority claim. It is a **different prospective numerical algorithm**
from the existing Taylor screen and
fixed absolute 128-bit references. All their original failures, flags and
criteria stay unchanged. No research program is declared closed.

The defining measure remains Makkeh–Gutknecht–Wibral (MGW) categorical
shared exclusions: [defining paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.103.032149),
[exact arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03356v5).
The [component derivation](POSITIVE_CATEGORICAL_COMPONENT_BOUNDS_2026_10_11.md)
and [count/sign argument](FINITE_COUNT_DIMENSION_AND_SIGN_2026_10_11.md)
fix its event, inverse and integer-product correspondence. The elementary
logarithm enclosure is useful beyond PID; it is not a new PID definition
or a claim to have invented arbitrary-precision interval arithmetic.

The product reduction and finite logarithm series already appear in the
[frozen 108-coordinate conventions](../claims/SX-CERTIFIED-AVERAGED-PID3-001/conventions.md).
The relative term bound here does not make those primitives new. Those
conventions write every cumulative and atom coordinate as an exact logarithm
of a positive rational, divided by the same positive row count. The numerical
argument applies to each such supplied ratio, including signed coordinates.
It does not establish their complete reconstruction, canonical byte parsing,
independent verification or compiled Rust refinement. The prepared sign-only
diagnostic targets 18 averaged atoms per law, not all 108 coordinates.

## 1. Assumptions and every input symbol

Sources \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) and target \(T\) have finite categorical
alphabets; \(n\) is their finite source count. A finite law has occupied
joint anchors indexed by \(s\), positive integer multiplicities \(c_s\),
and row count \(N=\sum_s c_s>0\). Frequencies are these counts divided by
their total, not rounded real probabilities. Fix an ordinary MGW antichain
\(\alpha\): a nonempty family of nonempty source-position subsets with
no subset contained in another. Its event uses AND within a branch and OR
between branches, always relative to the chosen anchor's labels.

Let \(R_s^+,R_s^-\) be that anchor/node's exact positive rational
logarithm arguments for the informative and misinformative components.
The first uses the source marginal; the second uses sources conditional
on the anchor's target label. Occupied anchors ensure all required event
probabilities are positive. Local signed atom is
\(\sigma_s=\ln R_s^+-\ln R_s^-\), in nats. Both sides use the **same
marginal anchor weight** \(c_s/N\) when averaged.

No observed-source independence, Gaussian/continuous/angular support,
independent and identically distributed rows, stationarity or population
calibration assumption is needed for the finite arithmetic. The law,
encoding, target conditioning, row alignment and ordinary inverse identity
must nevertheless be correct. Exact arithmetic does not repair a wrong
estimand. Irrational weights and binary64-only probabilities are outside
this integer-count contract.

## 2. Remove cancellation before logarithms

Write each signed ratio as \(R_s^+/R_s^-=a_s/b_s\), with positive
integers \(a_s,b_s\). Greatest-common-divisor reduction is optional for
the identity, useful for cost. Define positive integer products

\[
P=\prod_s a_s^{c_s},\qquad Q=\prod_s b_s^{c_s}.
\]

The average, denoted \(A\), satisfies exactly

\[
A=\sum_s\frac{c_s}{N}\sigma_s
=\frac1N\sum_s c_s\ln(a_s/b_s)
=\frac1N\ln(P/Q).
\]

The last equality uses integer exponents and the logarithm product rule.
Comparing \(P,Q\) decides the sign: positive for \(P>Q\), negative for
\(P<Q\), exactly zero for equality. At equality return the exact interval
\([0,0]\); relative error has no zero denominator and is not a success
percentage. Do not confuse net zero with zero nonnegative components.

For \(P\ne Q\), define \(R=\max(P,Q)/\min(P,Q)>1\), and retain the
sign separately. Then \(|A|=\ln R/N\). Range-reduce using exact integer
comparisons: find integer \(e\ge0\) with \(2^e\le R<2^{e+1}\), and
put \(v=R/2^e\), so \(1\le v<2\). Thus

\[
\ln R=e\ln2+\ln v.
\]

The symbol \(v\) is a reduced logarithm argument, not variance or velocity.
Every term on the right is nonnegative. There is no subtraction of large
logarithms in this evaluation route.

## 3. Finite logarithm identity and its tail

For a rational argument \(v\in[1,2]\), put
\(z=(v-1)/(v+1)\), so \(0\le z\le1/3\) and
\(v=(1+z)/(1-z)\). Let \(q\ge1\) be the fixed integer term count and
\(j\) a term index. Define the rational lower sum \(\ell_q\) and
tail ceiling \(\tau_q\):

\[
\ell_q(z)=2\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1},\qquad
\tau_q(z)=\frac{2z^{2q+1}}{(2q+1)(1-z^2)}.
\]

No infinite-series exchange is needed to prove the enclosure. The finite
geometric identity, for integration coordinate \(t\in[0,z]\), is

\[
\frac1{1-t^2}=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}t^{2j}+\frac{t^{2q}}{1-t^2}.
\]

Since \(\ln v=2\int_0^z(1-t^2)^{-1}dt\), integration gives

\[
\ln v=\ell_q(z)+2\int_0^z\frac{t^{2q}}{1-t^2}dt.
\]

The integrand is nonnegative, and \(1-t^2\ge1-z^2>0\). Integrating
the majorant yields

\[
0\le\ln v-\ell_q(z)\le\tau_q(z).
\]

For \(z>0\), the first retained term gives \(\ell_q(z)\ge2z\).
Consequently

\[
\frac{\tau_q(z)}{\ell_q(z)}
\le\frac{z^{2q}}{(2q+1)(1-z^2)}
\le\rho_q,\qquad
\rho_q=\frac9{8(2q+1)3^{2q}}.
\]

The second inequality uses \(z\le1/3\): both \(z^{2q}\) and
\(1/(1-z^2)\) increase with \(z\). At \(z=0\), both quantities are
zero; use that exact branch, not division by zero. The symbol \(\rho_q\)
is a uniform relative tail-to-lower-sum ceiling.

## 4. Enclose the whole average, including negative signs

For the reduced \(v\), use its \(z\); for \(\ln2\), use \(1/3\).
Define raw exact rational endpoints

\[
L=e\ell_q(1/3)+\ell_q(z),\qquad
U=L+e\tau_q(1/3)+\tau_q(z).
\]

Then \(L\le\ln R\le U\). Because \(R>1\), either \(e>0\) or
\(z>0\), so \(L>0\). Each tail is at most \(\rho_q\) times its
retained lower sum; multiplication by \(e\ge0\) and addition preserve
that inequality. Therefore

\[
U-L\le\rho_q L.
\]

For a positive average return \([L/N,U/N]\); for a negative average
return \([-U/N,-L/N]\). Let \(C\) be the returned interval's exact
midpoint. Containment and \(|A|\ge L/N\) give

\[
\frac{|C-A|}{|A|}
\le\frac{(U-L)/(2N)}{L/N}
\le\frac{\rho_q}{2}
=\frac9{16(2q+1)3^{2q}}.
\]

The factor \(N\) cancels. No lower bound on \(|A|\) enters the proof.
For fixed term counts:

| Terms \(q\) | Uniform midpoint relative ceiling | Declared threshold it meets |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | \(1/48\) | Not a universal 1% certificate |
| 2 | \(1/720\) | 1% |
| 5 | \(1/1154736\) | Four ppm; also below one ppm |
| 64 | \(9/(2064\,3^{128})<2^{-199}\) | Much smaller, at extra bit cost |

For the final strict comparison, \(3^{128}>2^{192}\) and
\(9\cdot128<2064\). These are hand integer comparisons, not executed
formal checks. Ppm here means relative numerical error, not confidence or
sensor concentration.

The one-term limitation has a realizable example, not just a loose bound.
Take two rows, \((000,T=0)\) and \((111,T=1)\), each of count one,
and ordinary bottom: at least one source matches the anchor. All three
sources are identical binary labels.
Each marginal event has probability \(1/2\); conditional on its target
the event is certain. Thus each local signed atom is \(\ln2\), and
\(A=\ln2\), \(N=2\), \(P=4\), \(Q=1\). Range reduction gives
\(e=2,v=1,z=0\). For \(q=1\),
\(\ell_1(1/3)=2/3\), \(\tau_1(1/3)=1/36\). Thus
\(L=4/3\), \(U=25/18\); division by \(N=2\) returns
\([2/3,25/36]\), whose midpoint is \(C=49/72\). Three retained terms give
\(\ln2\ge2/3+2/81+2/1215=842/1215\). The exact comparison
\(100\cdot49\cdot1215=5953500<6001776=99\cdot842\cdot72\)
implies \(C<(99/100)\ln2\): its relative error exceeds 1%.
This hand negative control leaves the uniform two- and five-term guarantees
unchanged; it is not an executed new fixture.

## 5. Fully specified losing-law worked example

Use the earlier count-dropping control law: all eight binary source triples.
At 000 and 111 the count is three and target zero. At 001,010,011 the count
is one and target zero; at 110,101,100 it is one and target one. Then
\(N=12\), target counts are nine and three. Use ordinary bottom: at least
one source matches the anchor.

At either high-count anchor, only its bitwise complement fails this event.
The marginal accepted count is \(12-3=9\), so \(R^+=12/9=4/3\).
Within its nine-row target pool the accepted count is \(9-3=6\), so
\(R^-=9/6=3/2\). Its signed ratio is \((4/3)/(3/2)=8/9\).
At each low-count anchor, the marginal accepted count is \(12-1=11\).
Its complement has the other target label; the conditional event is certain.
Thus \(R^+=12/11\), \(R^-=1\), and the signed ratio is \(12/11\).
The two high-count anchors together supply exponent \(3+3=6\); the
six low-count anchors together supply exponent six. Correct multiplicities give

\[
P/Q=(8/9)^6(12/11)^6=(32/33)^6,
\qquad A=\tfrac12\ln(32/33)<0.
\]

Dropping multiplicities reverses the sign, and substituting conditional
anchor weights also reverses it; those failures remain. For the correct
negative average, \(R=(33/32)^6=1291467969/1073741824<2\), so \(e=0\).
The reduced series argument is

\[
z=\frac{217726145}{2365209793}<\frac1{10}.
\]

With only one term, \(\ell_1=2z\) and
\(\tau_1=2z^3/[3(1-z^2)]\). The returned interval is
\([-(\ell_1+\tau_1)/12,-\ell_1/12]\). Its specific relative ceiling is

\[
\frac{\tau_1}{2\ell_1}
=\frac{z^2}{6(1-z^2)}<\frac1{594}<1\%.
\]

This is a hand worked example, not an executed new native fixture. One
term succeeds here without being a universal 1% choice. Five terms would
give the preceding uniform ceiling without outcome-dependent selection.

## 6. Rust implementation and computational cost

No current Rust API or research sign producer implements this magnitude
certificate. The sign producer is separately bounded and must not acquire
this algorithm by an unnoticed source change. Its caps, old outputs and
criteria remain fixed. Formal/tool qualification and numerical refinement
are separate obligations. See the [implementation baseline](../RUST_IMPLEMENTATION_AND_COST.md).

The exact products can be large. Under the three-source \(N\le31000\)
contract, using the direct count-product representatives for \(a_s,b_s\)
or their greatest-common-divisor reductions, their bit ceiling is
\(120N+1\le3720001\) each. Enlarging both sides by arbitrary common
factors preserves the ratio but invalidates this cost bound. A
fixed small term count does not make arbitrary-precision operations
constant-time. Series powers, rational additions and greatest-common-divisor
reductions can retain multiples of those bit lengths. With 64 terms,
degree-129 expressions can involve hundreds of millions of bits. Parsing,
carrier reconstruction, product powers and output storage cost separately.
No 180-second, 256-MiB or 8-MiB-output success follows from the theorem.

Raw rational endpoints are essential to this statement. Rounding them onto
the old fixed absolute grid can destroy relative precision near zero.
Binary64 conversion, exponent underflow or compact outward serialization
needs a separate error analysis; missing/out-of-budget results must abstain.
Never silently round a nonzero average to an exact-zero result.

Arbitrary-precision ball arithmetic is an established alternative, not an
unbeaten baseline: [FLINT's Arb documentation](https://flintlib.org/doc/arb.html)
describes enclosing arithmetic and a log1p function for arguments near zero.
A prospective comparator should form the exact integer difference before
approximating \((P-Q)/Q\), and use a qualified enclosing log1p route.
No Arb benchmark, superiority, new dependency or refinement is claimed.

## 7. Scientific meaning and open work

The [occupancy screen](EMPIRICAL_COMPONENT_BOUND_SCREEN_2026_10_11.md)
retains wide Taylor bounds and 55 sign-unresolved averages. This proposal
addresses numerical evaluation of their exact empirical quantity, not
population uncertainty or predictor quality. Exact signs and reliable
magnitudes could be useful audit references for a future Rust method or
for evaluating a justified diagnostic decision against direct task loss.
That application benefit has not been measured.

Neural, imaging and embodied studies require justified categorical variables,
actual aligned outcomes and dependence-aware uncertainty. Generated forecasts
are not observed future targets. Nothing here transfers categorical MGW to
continuous shared exclusions, angular or hyperbolic estimators, or I_min.

The earlier fixed-grid dimension obstruction and weak signed-average
separation gap remain valid: they limit that absolute-grid route, not every
possible representation. New source, exact arithmetic/refinement,
stock formal acceptance, resource measurements, qualified matching
PDF and application comparators remain open. SxPID3 A–E, original formal
targets, angular, hyperbolic and v1 are not closed by this proposed route.
