11 October 2026. Mathematical report prepared for integration review.

For a supported categorical observation, a shared-exclusions component
atom can be written as a positive box integral. This gives an exact zero
criterion, elementary bounds, symmetric midpoint refinements, and a
convergent series for every finite vector of relative masses. None of
these statements gives relative accuracy for the signed difference of
two components, an outward floating-point certificate, or evidence of
predictive benefit.

The measure is due to Makkeh, Gutknecht and Wibral,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.103.032149}{Physical Review E
103, 032149 (2021)}, with
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03356v5}{arXiv version 5}. These define
the measure, not bound priority. Correspondence uses the retained
\href{https://github.com/sepahead/pid-rs/blob/main/claims/SX-CERTIFIED-AVERAGED-PID3-001/source-correspondence-v4.md}{source-correspondence
record}; no fresh paper reading is claimed.

\section{1. Events, laws, components, and
units}\label{events-laws-components-and-units}

Let \(N=\{1,\ldots,n\}\) be a nonempty finite set of source indices.
Each \(X_i\) is a categorical source with a finite alphabet, and \(T\)
is a categorical target. Fix an anchor \((s_1,\ldots,s_n,t)\) with
strictly positive joint probability under the declared law
\(\mathbb P\). Therefore \(\mathbb P(T=t)>0\).

An ordinary node \(\alpha\) is a nonempty family of nonempty subsets of
\(N\), with no member containing another. Such a family is called an
antichain. Its event is

\[
E_\alpha=\bigcup_{D\in\alpha}\ \bigcap_{i\in D}\{X_i=s_i\}.
\]

Thus a branch requires agreement at every source it names; the node
accepts agreement with at least one branch. No independence assumption
is made.

There are two source laws: \(Q_+\) is the unconditional law of
\((X_i)_{i\in N}\), and \(Q_-\) is its law conditional on \(T=t\). For
either law \(Q\), a source row \(y\) has agreement pattern
\(B(y)=\{i:y_i=s_i\}\). Write \(q_B=Q(B(y)=B)\) for each
\(B\subseteq N\). These disjoint cell masses are nonnegative and sum to
one. The supported anchor ensures \(q_N>0\) under both laws.

The covered patterns and their mass are

\[
U_\alpha=\{B\subseteq N:\text{some }D\in\alpha\text{ satisfies }D\subseteq B\},
\qquad u=Q(E_\alpha)=\sum_{B\in U_\alpha}q_B>0.
\]

Set \(F_Q(\alpha)=-\ln u\). All logarithms here are natural: quantities
are in nats. A source statement in bits must be multiplied by \(\ln 2\)
before comparison.

The redundancy order is \(\alpha\le\beta\) when
\(U_\beta\subseteq U_\alpha\). Define component atoms by the finite
inverse relation

\[
F_Q(\alpha)=\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}\pi_Q(\beta).
\]

Bayes' rule gives the signed shared-exclusions redundancy:
\(\ln[\mathbb P(T=t\mid E_\alpha)/\mathbb P(T=t)]
=\ln[Q_-(E_\alpha)/Q_+(E_\alpha)]=F_{Q_+}(\alpha)-F_{Q_-}(\alpha)\). By
linearity of the inverse, its signed atom is
\(\pi_{Q_+}(\alpha)-\pi_{Q_-}(\alpha)\). The two components are
nonnegative below; their difference need not be.

\section{2. Why only maximal uncovered patterns enter the
inverse}\label{why-only-maximal-uncovered-patterns-enter-the-inverse}

Fix \(\alpha\) and \(Q\). Let \(H_\alpha=2^N\setminus U_\alpha\), where
\(2^N\) denotes all subsets of \(N\). This is downward closed: if a
pattern is uncovered, every smaller pattern is uncovered. It contains
\(\varnothing\) but not \(N\). Let \(B_1,\ldots,B_k\) be its maximal
members, meaning that no larger member of \(H_\alpha\) contains them.
Define

\[
k\ge1,\qquad a_i=q_{B_i}\ge0,\qquad A=\sum_{i=1}^k a_i,\qquad
C=(k-1)!\prod_{i=1}^k a_i.
\]

The cells are distinct and outside \(U_\alpha\), so \(u+A\le1\).

For the inverse proof, remove the two endpoint patterns and form
\(\mathcal K=2^N\setminus\{\varnothing,N\}\). An ideal of \(\mathcal K\)
is a subset containing every smaller member of \(\mathcal K\) whenever
it contains a member. The set
\(J_\alpha=H_\alpha\setminus\{\varnothing\}\) is an ideal.

Conversely, from any ideal \(J\), form \(H=J\cup\{\varnothing\}\) and
\(U=2^N\setminus H\). The minimal members of \(U\) are a nonempty
antichain of nonempty subsets. Every member of \(U\) contains a minimal
member, by finite descent. This reconstructs the original covered
family. Consequently ordinary nodes and ideals are in bijection, and
\(\alpha\le\beta\) exactly when \(J_\alpha\subseteq J_\beta\).

For any function \(F\) on these ideals, define

\[
g(J)=\sum_{D\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(J)}(-1)^{|D|}F(J\setminus D).
\]

Deleting maximal members leaves an ideal. To prove
\(F(J)=\sum_{I\subseteq J,\ I\ {\rm ideal}}g(I)\), fix an ideal
\(L\subseteq J\) and collect the coefficient of \(F(L)\). A contributing
term has \(I=L\cup D\) and \(D\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(I)\). Such
\(D\) are exactly the subsets of \(\operatorname{Min}(J\setminus L)\):

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  If \(d\in D\) had a smaller member \(x\in J\setminus L\), the ideal
  property would place \(x\) in \(D\), contradicting maximality of its
  members.
\item
  Conversely these minimal members are incomparable, all their
  predecessors in \(J\) lie in \(L\), and none can lie below a member of
  \(L\); otherwise the ideal \(L\) would already contain it. Adding any
  subset therefore gives an ideal with the added members maximal.
\end{itemize}

For \(L=J\) the coefficient is one. Otherwise the finite nonempty set
\(J\setminus L\) has \(r\ge1\) minimal members, and its coefficient is
\(\sum_D(-1)^{|D|}=(1-1)^r=0\). This proves the inverse. It is unique by
solving successively for ideals of increasing size.

If \(J_\alpha\) is nonempty, its maximal members equal those of
\(H_\alpha\). Deleting selected maximal uncovered patterns adds exactly
their disjoint cells to the event. Applying the inverse to \(F_Q\) gives
the localized row

\[
\pi_Q(\alpha)=
\sum_{D\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}}(-1)^{|D|}
\left[-\ln\left(u+\sum_{i\in D}a_i\right)\right].
\tag{1}
\]

The ordinary bottom node contains all singleton branches. Here
\(H_\alpha=\{\varnothing\}\), \(J_\alpha\) is empty, and the ordinary
inverse has just \(-\ln(1-q_\varnothing)\). Formula (1) still works with
\(k=1\), \(a_1=q_\varnothing\), \(u=1-a_1\), because its additional term
is \(-\ln(u+a_1)=-\ln1=0\).

That zero term corresponds to a virtual certain event, the family
\(\{\varnothing\}\) containing an empty branch. Its conjunction is true,
its covered mass is one, and its cumulative value is zero. It is not an
ordinary node in the bijection above. It is not the literal empty family
\(\varnothing\): an empty disjunction is false, has mass zero, and gives
the nonfinite value \(-\ln0\). For \(n=1\), the sole ordinary atom is
simply \(-\ln q_N\).

\section{3. Turning alternating logarithms into a positive
integral}\label{turning-alternating-logarithms-into-a-positive-integral}

Let \(f(x)=-\ln x\) for \(x>0\). For a nonnegative width \(a\) define
the difference \(D_a f(x)=f(x)-f(x+a)\). The fundamental theorem of
calculus gives \(D_a f(x)=-\int_0^a f'(x+w)\,dw\).

The product of these commuting differences expands to the subset sum in
(1). Applying the same identity \(k\) times gives \((-1)^k\) times the
box integral of \(f^{(k)}(u+\sum_i w_i)\). Differentiating, or inducting
from \(f'(x)=-1/x\), gives \(f^{(k)}(x)=(-1)^k(k-1)!x^{-k}\). Thus,
writing \(P=\pi_Q(\alpha)\),

\[
P=(k-1)!\int_0^{a_1}\cdots\int_0^{a_k}
       (u+w_1+\cdots+w_k)^{-k}\,dw_k\cdots dw_1.
\tag{2}
\]

The integrand is positive and bounded because \(u>0\). At \(u=0\), even
\(k=1\) with positive width gives a divergent integral; no extension is
claimed. If some \(a_i=0\), the box has zero volume and \(P=0\). If all
\(a_i>0\), its volume is positive and \(P>0\). Hence

\[
P=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{at least one maximal uncovered pattern has zero mass under }Q.
\tag{3}
\]

This concerns the declared law; an unobserved sample cell does not imply
a population zero. An event may have positive cumulative surprisal while
this particular inverse component vanishes. The proof uses the full
categorical antichain carrier and its ideal representation; an arbitrary
event poset does not inherit it.

\section{4. Rectangle and one-pattern bounds; what rarity
means}\label{rectangle-and-one-pattern-bounds-what-rarity-means}

Throughout the box, \(u\le u+\sum_i w_i\le u+A\). Multiplying endpoint
bounds by its volume gives

\[
\frac{C}{(u+A)^k}\le P\le\frac{C}{u^k}.
\tag{4}
\]

Introduce relative masses \(r_i=a_i/u\), their sum \(R=A/u\), and
\(G=(k-1)!\prod_i r_i=C/u^k\). Then (4) reads \(G/(1+R)^k\le P\le G\).
These expressions are unchanged if \(u\) and every \(a_i\) are
multiplied by the same positive number; scaled numerical variables need
not themselves be probabilities.

For a one-pattern bound, take \(x>0\). Substitution followed by \(k-1\)
integrations by parts gives
\(\int_0^\infty z^{k-1}e^{-xz}\,dz=(k-1)!x^{-k}\). Insert this identity
into (2). Nonnegative integrands allow the order of integration to be
exchanged. Each box integral contributes \((1-e^{-a_i z})/z\), leaving

\[
P=\int_0^\infty e^{-uz}\prod_{i=1}^k(1-e^{-a_i z})\,\frac{dz}{z}.
\]

For any index \(j\), dropping all factors except \(j\) increases the
integrand because each factor lies in \([0,1]\). Use
\((1-e^{-a_j z})/z=\int_0^{a_j}e^{-wz}\,dw\) and integrate first in
\(z\):

\[
P\le\int_0^{a_j}\frac{dw}{u+w}
   =\ln(1+a_j/u),\qquad
P\le\min_j\ln(1+r_j).
\tag{5}
\]

Therefore a relative mass \(r_j\to0\) forces \(P\to0\). Absolute rarity
alone does not. For two binary sources at the top node \(\{\{1,2\}\}\),
assign \(q_{\{1,2\}}=q_{\{1\}}=q_{\{2\}}=\varepsilon\) and
\(q_\varnothing=1-3\varepsilon\), with \(0<\varepsilon<1/3\). Then
\(u=a_1=a_2=\varepsilon\), but

\[
P=\ln\frac{(u+a_1)(u+a_2)}{u(u+a_1+a_2)}=\ln(4/3).
\tag{6}
\]

All three small masses tend to zero while \(r_1=r_2=1\). In contrast, if
\(u\ge\eta>0\) for a fixed covered-mass floor \(\eta\), then \(a_j\to0\)
implies \(r_j\le a_j/\eta\to0\) and (5) applies.

\section{5. Auxiliary uniforms and the second-order midpoint
interval}\label{auxiliary-uniforms-and-the-second-order-midpoint-interval}

Handle (3) first; suppose now all widths are positive. Introduce
independent auxiliary variables \(Z_i\) uniformly distributed on
\([0,a_i]\), and set \(W=\sum_i Z_i\) and \(h(w)=(u+w)^{-k}\). Dividing
the box integral by its volume gives

\[
P=C\,\mathbb E[h(W)].
\tag{7}
\]

These uniforms are integration weights, not a model for the observed
sources or sample rows. They do not require the \(X_i\) to be
independent or uniform.

Direct integration gives \(\mathbb E Z_i=a_i/2\) and
\(\mathbb E Z_i^2=a_i^2/3\), hence \(\operatorname{Var}(Z_i)=a_i^2/12\).
Auxiliary independence makes cross-covariances zero. Define

\[
\mu=A/2,\qquad \delta=W-\mu,\qquad
\sigma^2=\mathbb E\delta^2=\sum_i a_i^2/12,\qquad \mathbb E\delta=0.
\]

The second derivative is \(h''(w)=k(k+1)(u+w)^{-k-2}\), positive and
decreasing on \([0,A]\). Taylor's formula with an integral remainder is

\[
h(\mu+\delta)=h(\mu)+h'(\mu)\delta+
\delta^2\int_0^1(1-\lambda)h''(\mu+\lambda\delta)\,d\lambda.
\]

The entire interpolation segment lies in \([0,A]\). Bound the derivative
by \(h''(A)\) and \(h''(0)\), take expectations, and use
\(\int_0^1(1-\lambda)\,d\lambda=1/2\). The linear term vanishes.
Consequently

\[
L_2=C\left[h(\mu)+\frac{h''(A)\sigma^2}{2}\right]
\le P\le
U_2=C\left[h(\mu)+\frac{h''(0)\sigma^2}{2}\right].
\tag{8}
\]

For any genuine interval \(0<L\le P\le U\), define its relative
full-width ceiling to be \((U-L)/L\). Then \((U-L)/P\le(U-L)/L\), and
its midpoint has relative error at most \((U-L)/(2L)\). These are
ceilings, not exact errors divided by \(P\). Relative divisions are
undefined at a structural zero.

\section{6. Symmetry and fourth-order
refinements}\label{symmetry-and-fourth-order-refinements}

Each \(Z_i-a_i/2\) is symmetric about zero. Independence makes their sum
\(\delta\) symmetric, so \(\mathbb E\delta=\mathbb E\delta^3=0\). Write
\(m_4=\mathbb E\delta^4\). A centered uniform has fourth moment
\(a_i^4/80\). Expanding the fourth power of the sum, odd cross terms
vanish and pairs have coefficient six. Therefore

\[
m_4=\sum_i\frac{a_i^4}{80}
+\sum_{i<j}\frac{a_i^2a_j^2}{24}
=3(\sigma^2)^2-\sum_i\frac{a_i^4}{120}.
\]

Let \(g(w)=h^{(4)}(w)=k(k+1)(k+2)(k+3)(u+w)^{-k-4}\) and
\(M_2=h(\mu)+h''(\mu)\sigma^2/2\). Taylor expansion through degree three
has remainder
\(\delta^4\int_0^1(1-\lambda)^3g(\mu+\lambda\delta)\,d\lambda/6\). Its
weight integrates to \(1/24\). Since \(g\) is decreasing, this gives the
valid raw interval

\[
L_4=C[M_2+g(A)m_4/24]\le P\le
U_4=C[M_2+g(0)m_4/24].
\tag{9}
\]

Symmetry improves this interval further. The function \(g\) is convex
because \(g''=h^{(6)}>0\). For \(0\le z\le A/2\), convexity gives

\[
g(\mu)\le\frac{g(\mu-z)+g(\mu+z)}2
\le\frac{g(0)+g(A)}2.
\]

The first inequality is the midpoint convexity inequality. For the
second, bound both values by the chord between endpoints; their
complementary chord weights add to one. Pair \(\delta\) and \(-\delta\)
in the remainder and apply this inequality to \(z=\lambda|\delta|\). The
factor \(\delta^4\) is unchanged. Thus

\[
L_{4s}=C[M_2+g(\mu)m_4/24]\le P\le
U_{4s}=C[M_2+(g(0)+g(A))m_4/48].
\tag{10}
\]

In particular \(L_4\le L_{4s}\le P\le U_{4s}\le U_4\). The fourth-order
lower endpoint improves the second-order lower endpoint, but a
fourth-order upper endpoint need not improve \(U_2\).

Here is an admissible counterexample. Take two-source top-node masses
\(u=1/5\), \(a_1=a_2=2/5\), and \(q_\varnothing=0\). Multiply these
three cover variables by five solely to calculate the scale-invariant
expressions: \(u'=1\), \(a'_1=a'_2=2\). These primed variables are not a
probability law. They give \(A'=4\), \(\mu'=2\), \(\sigma'^2=2/3\),
\(m'_4=16/15\), \(C'=4\). Equations (8) and (9) yield

\[
U_2=76/9,\qquad U_4=1772/81>76/9,\qquad P=\ln(9/5).
\tag{11}
\]

Thus raw fourth order is not uniformly tighter. Valid intervals can
instead be intersected: take the largest lower endpoint and smallest
upper endpoint from (4), (5), (8), and (10), using exact or genuinely
outward arithmetic.

Symmetry is essential, not optional. Replacing the auxiliary law by a
variable \(V\) taking \(0\) and \(1/15\) with probabilities \(1/4\) and
\(3/4\) gives mean \(1/20\), variance \(1/1200\), third centered moment
\(-1/36000\), and fourth centered moment \(7/4320000\). For
\(h(w)=(1+w)^{-1}\), its exact expectation is \(61/64\). Dropping the
odd term as if this law were symmetric gives the false upper expression
\(4236807203/4445280000\), below the expectation by
\(100297/4445280000\). This is an outside-domain countermodel, not a
failure of the integration uniforms.

\section{7. An even series for every finite relative mass
vector}\label{an-even-series-for-every-finite-relative-mass-vector}

Keep \(u>0\), finite \(a_i\ge0\), and handle zero volume separately. Set

\[
b=u+\mu,\quad v=\delta/b,\quad
\rho=\frac{A}{2u+A}=\frac{R}{2+R}<1,\quad
K=C b^{-k},\quad \nu_{2j}=\mathbb E[v^{2j}].
\]

Here \(j\) is a nonnegative integer, \(\nu_0=1\), and \(|v|\le\rho\).
Multiplying \(k\) absolutely convergent geometric series gives
\((1+v)^{-k}=\sum_{\ell\ge0}(-1)^\ell\binom{k+\ell-1}{\ell}v^\ell\). The
coefficient counts ways to split \(\ell\) among \(k\) nonnegative
integers, equivalently placements of \(k-1\) separators. On
\([-\rho,\rho]\) the absolute sum is bounded by \((1-\rho)^{-k}\).
Uniform convergence therefore permits expectation term by term. Symmetry
removes the odd powers:

\[
P=K\sum_{j=0}^\infty d_j\nu_{2j},
\qquad d_j=\binom{k+2j-1}{2j}.
\tag{12}
\]

Every term is nonnegative. For a finite order \(m\ge0\), let
\(S_m=K\sum_{j=0}^m d_j\nu_{2j}\). These lower bounds increase to \(P\).
In particular \(S_2=L_{4s}\).

A finite closed tail bound follows from \(\nu_{2j}\le\rho^{2j}\). The
even part of the same geometric product has the exact sum

\[
E_k(\rho)=\frac{(1-\rho)^{-k}+(1+\rho)^{-k}}2,\qquad
H_m=E_k(\rho)-\sum_{j=0}^m d_j\rho^{2j}\ge0.
\]

Hence, for every \(\rho<1\),

\[
0\le P-S_m\le K H_m.
\tag{13}
\]

This finite formula can lose precision by floating-point subtraction;
exact algebra does not make its rounded evaluation outward.

There is also a geometric tail. Define \(\beta_j=d_j\rho^{2j}\). For
\(0<\rho<1\), successive ratios are

\[
\theta_j=\frac{\beta_{j+1}}{\beta_j}
=\rho^2\left(1+\frac{k-1}{2j+1}\right)
       \left(1+\frac{k-1}{2j+2}\right).
\]

They are nonincreasing and tend to \(\rho^2<1\). If \(\theta_{m+1}<1\),
then

\[
P-S_m\le K\,\frac{\beta_{m+1}}{1-\theta_{m+1}}.
\tag{14}
\]

If this early ratio is at least one, (14) must not be used. Choose an
integer

\[
J\ge\max\left\{m+1,\
\left\lceil\frac{(k-1)\rho}{2(1-\rho)}\right\rceil\right\}.
\]

Then \(2J+1>(k-1)\rho/(1-\rho)\), so
\(\theta_J\le\rho^2[1+(k-1)/(2J+1)]^2<1\). A finite prefix plus a
geometric tail gives

\[
P-S_m\le K\left[
\sum_{j=m+1}^{J-1}\beta_j+\frac{\beta_J}{1-\theta_J}\right].
\tag{15}
\]

An empty prefix is zero. For \(\rho=0\) all widths vanish and the
structural-zero branch applies; ratios need not be formed.

Convergence is pointwise for each finite mass vector, not a uniform
fixed-order accuracy guarantee. For \(k=1\), write \(r=a_1/u\). Then
\(\rho=r/(2+r)\) and \(P=\ln(1+r)=2\sum_{j\ge0}\rho^{2j+1}/(2j+1)\). As
\(r\to\infty\), \(\rho\to1\), the exact \(P\) diverges, but each fixed
\(S_m\) tends to a finite sum. Its relative error tends to one. An
admissible law has \(u=1/(1+r)\) and \(a_1=r/(1+r)\). Narrow intervals
can require a large order or another route. Many small \(r_i\) can still
have a large sum \(R\); eligibility is component-specific, not assumed
for every dataset.

\section{8. Computing moments and counting the
work}\label{computing-moments-and-counting-the-work}

Let \(Y_i=Z_i-a_i/2\). Direct integration gives
\(\mathbb E Y_i^{2\ell}=(a_i/2)^{2\ell}/(2\ell+1)\) and zero odd
moments. For a chosen order \(m\), define factor coefficients
\(q_{i,\ell}=(a_i/2)^{2\ell}/(2\ell+1)!\) for \(0\le\ell\le m\). The
multinomial expansion and auxiliary independence give

\[
\mathbb E\delta^{2j}=(2j)!\,[z^j]\prod_{i=1}^k
\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^m q_{i,\ell}z^\ell\right),\qquad 0\le j\le m,
\]

where \([z^j]\) means the coefficient of \(z^j\) and \(z\) is a formal
variable, not an observation. Divide by \(b^{2j}\) to obtain
\(\nu_{2j}\).

For \(m\ge1\), the recurrence \(q_{i,0}=1\) and
\(q_{i,\ell+1}=q_{i,\ell}(a_i/2)^2/[(2\ell+2)(2\ell+3)]\) generates all
\(k\) length-\((m+1)\) vectors in \(O(km)\) elementary arithmetic
operations. Moment-factorial generation costs \(O(m)\); reading the
masses and forming their prefactor costs \(O(k)\). Naive sequential
convolution truncated at degree \(m\) costs \(O(km^2)\) arithmetic
operations and needs \(O(m+k)\) stored scalar values if factors are
generated one at a time. At \(m=0\), only initialization is needed. This
is not a rational bit-complexity or measured runtime bound.

Once moments are available, (13) adds \(O(m)\) operations and two
integer powers, computable in \(O(\log k)\) multiplications. Formula
(15) adds \(O(J-m)\) recurrence steps; \(J\) can be large near
\(\rho=1\). Fixed second/fourth order needs \(O(k)\) arithmetic from
supplied masses. This excludes constructing masses, the carrier, and all
anchors.

\section{9. Assigned count law: four parts per million and a much
narrower
interval}\label{assigned-count-law-four-parts-per-million-and-a-much-narrower-interval}

Take two binary sources, anchor \((0,0)\), and a constant target
\(T=0\). Assign exactly these 100 rows:

{\def\LTcaptype{none} % do not increment counter
\begin{longtable}[]{@{}llr@{}}
\toprule\noalign{}
Source row & Agreement pattern & Count \\
\midrule\noalign{}
\endhead
\bottomrule\noalign{}
\endlastfoot
\((0,0)\) & \(\{1,2\}\) & 80 \\
\((0,1)\) & \(\{1\}\) & 1 \\
\((1,0)\) & \(\{2\}\) & 1 \\
\((1,1)\) & \(\varnothing\) & 18 \\
\end{longtable}
}

At the top node \(\alpha=\{\{1,2\}\}\), only full agreement is covered.
The maximal uncovered patterns are the two singletons, not the empty
pattern. Thus \(u=80/100\), \(a_1=a_2=1/100\), \(k=2\), and

\[
P=-\ln(80/100)+2\ln(81/100)-\ln(82/100)
=\ln\frac{81^2}{80\cdot82}=\ln(6561/6560).
\tag{16}
\]

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=1\linewidth,height=\textheight,keepaspectratio,alt={Agreement cells and the four masses in the two-source inverse row. Cell areas do not represent probabilities.}]{component-cover-masses-20261011.pdf}
\caption{Agreement cells and the four masses in the two-source inverse
row. Cell areas do not represent probabilities.}
\end{figure}

Here \(r_1=r_2=1/80\). The rectangle gives \(1/6724\le P\le1/6400\). Its
geometric midpoint is \(1/6560\), with relative-error ceiling
\(1/40=2.5\%\): the largest endpoint-relative error is its ratio to the
lower endpoint minus one.

For sharper arithmetic, divide all three cover variables by \(u\). The
resulting \(u'=1\), \(a'_1=a'_2=1/80\) are normalized calculation
variables, not a probability law. They give \(A'=1/40\), \(\mu'=1/80\),
\(b'=81/80\), \(C'=1/6400\), \(\sigma'^2=1/38400\),
\(m'_4=1/614400000\), and \(\rho=1/81\). Equation (8) gives

\[
L_2=\frac1{6561}+\frac1{32\cdot41^4},\qquad
U_2=\frac1{6561}+\frac1{81920000}.
\]

The midpoint relative-error ceiling is exactly

\[
\frac{U_2-L_2}{2L_2}
=\frac{1743657921}{463006274560000}<4\cdot10^{-6}.
\tag{17}
\]

The strict inequality is the integer comparison
\(1743657921\cdot250000=435914480250000<463006274560000\). This fixture
concerns approximation error, not calibration.

Equal widths give an especially useful exact moment law. Write
\(a=a'_1=a'_2\). The sum of the two centered uniforms has density
\((a-|d|)/a^2\) for \(|d|\le a\), obtained by the overlap length of
their two uniform intervals. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E\delta^{2j}=\frac2{a^2}\int_0^a d^{2j}(a-d)\,dd
=\frac{2a^{2j}}{(2j+1)(2j+2)}.
\]

For \(k=2\), \(d_j=2j+1\) in (12), \(a/b'=\rho\), and \(K=\rho^2\). Put
\(x=\rho^2=1/6561\). Cancellation of these factors in (12) gives

\[
P=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^\ell}{\ell}=-\ln(1-x),\qquad
L=S_2=x+x^2/2+x^3/3.
\]

Since \(1/\ell\le1/4\) for \(\ell\ge4\), the tail is at most
\(D=x^4/[4(1-x)]\). Set \(U=L+D\). The positive lower endpoint satisfies
\(L\ge x\), so

\[
\frac{U-L}{P}\le\frac{U-L}{L}
\le\frac{D}{x}
=\frac1{4\cdot6561^2\cdot6560}
=\frac1{1129545959040}<10^{-12}.
\tag{18}
\]

The midpoint relative-error ceiling is half the displayed full-width
ceiling. This sub-\(10^{-12}\) derivation is exact hand mathematics; it
is not a binary64 error estimate.

Later finite checks support this transcription. The root ran the frozen
rational helper once normally and once with Python optimization: both
exited zero and produced identical 11,544-byte JSON, while both
unexpected-argument controls exited one. Six fixed mass vectors check
moments and tail expressions: the worked vector, broad equal widths,
unequal three widths, zero volume, slow radius, and four widths with the
early geometric ratio blocked. The skew replacement-law countermodel is
an additional calculation, not a dataset. The blocked case has early
ratio \(5500/3087\ge1\) and admits cutoff 30 with ratio
\(832000/833931<1\).

For (18), that finite output records the exact width divided by the
lower endpoint, \((U-L)/L=3/3388896144320\), not the exact error divided
by \(P\). It also brackets \(\ln(6561/6560)\) by the degree-eight and
degree-nine alternating sums for \(\ln(1+y)\) with \(y=1/6560\):
decreasing alternating terms make the even sum lower and odd sum upper.
Both fit inside \([L,U]\). This is an algebraically distinct reference
in the same helper and Fraction/interpreter route, not an independently
implemented check or a categorical-estimator replay. The general proofs
remain the arguments above, not an extrapolation from six cases.

Finally, the target is constant, so \(Q_+=Q_-\) at every supported
anchor. Unique inversion gives equal components at every node, and every
signed atom is zero. A positive value in (16) is not target synergy,
fault-prediction gain, or permission to remove a source.

\section{10. Existing Rust surface and prospective numerical
work}\label{existing-rust-surface-and-prospective-numerical-work}

The
\href{https://github.com/sepahead/pid-rs/blob/main/crates/pid-core/src/lib.rs}{public
export map} and
\href{https://github.com/sepahead/pid-rs/blob/main/crates/pid-core/src/sxpid.rs}{categorical
implementation} expose discrete\_sxpid\_n and its averaged, budgeted and
cancellation-aware variants in stable::categorical. They accept
DiscreteMatRef sources and target for two through four sources. The full
ordinary carriers have 4, 18 and 166 nodes. The all-finite-\(n\)
derivation does not extend this runtime API.

The inspected implementation constructs a joint count law, evaluates
cumulative informative and misinformative terms at occupied anchors and
carrier nodes, inverts each component separately, and averages weighted
atoms. Its ExactBinary64Accumulator sums supplied rounded binary64 terms
exactly; it does not undo earlier rounding of logarithms, products, or
inverse subtraction. This API does not implement the new certificates.

A separate private Rust prototype is prepared but UNEXECUTED. It accepts
already constructed cover masses with \(1\le k\le6\), not six observed
sources. It contains one/two-cover closed log1p routes and a
second-order Taylor-midpoint route, not the new fourth-order or series
algorithm. Before its structural-zero branch, it validates finite
nonnegative inputs, \(u>0\), represented mass normalization, supported
cover order, and the requested model budget. The normalization check
uses a fixed 17-word integer accumulator for represented binary64
dyadics. Scale failures, overflow, positive underflow, and unmet
approximation budgets are refusals, not silently zero atoms.

Its lower/upper expressions and model ceiling are ordinary uncertified
f64 evaluations. A zero truncation budget for a closed formula does not
mean zero library-logarithm or rounding error. Real-input conversion,
directed rounding, overflow/underflow behavior, compiler and platform
behavior, and all-input refinement still need evidence. There is no
compiled result, benchmark, or new kernel theorem here.

Given a correct mass vector, a fixed-order component expression costs
\(O(k)\); the moment route above costs \(O(km^2)\). For comparison, with
\(d\) encoded rows and total source-label width \(w\), a naive
one-anchor agreement scan costs \(O(dw)\) comparisons; \(w=n\) for
scalar sources. Repeating it for \(h\) supported anchors costs
\(O(hdw)\) before carrier work. These are scan counts, not
current-implementation performance claims. Counting, conditioning,
occupied support, maximal-pattern construction, complete carriers, and
output storage remain separate costs. A localized alternating row has
\(2^k\) terms; avoiding that row does not make a complete PID
linear-time.

If genuinely outward intervals for the informative and misinformative
components are \([L_+,U_+]\) and \([L_-,U_-]\), their signed difference
lies in \([L_+-U_-,\,U_+-L_-]\). Near cancellation this is an absolute
enclosure, not a relative signed-net guarantee. Never clamp the signed
difference to restore positivity.
