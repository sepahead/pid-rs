# Compact relative enclosures for exact empirical logarithms

Sepehr Mahmoudian · 11 October 2026

## Result, purpose and status

An exact empirical shared-exclusions average can be extremely small because
positive and negative local terms cancel. The [whole-ratio derivation](CANCELLATION_FREE_SIGNED_AVERAGE_2026_10_11.md)
removes that cancellation before logarithms and gives a raw rational relative
bound. Its remaining difficulty is computational: raising a huge denominator
to high powers may consume substantial memory.

The construction here avoids those powers. It rounds a reduced argument
outward using exact integer division, then evaluates a five-term polynomial
on small rational endpoints. A separate tiny-value branch keeps an exact
integer exponent instead of expanding it. Every nonzero result has a hand
midpoint relative ceiling below one part per million. Under the stated
three-source count cap, polynomial endpoint coefficients have a conservative
4,096-bit ceiling when formed by the specified common denominators.

This is hand/source-reviewed mathematical progress, not an implementation,
kernel theorem, runtime/RSS result, human verification, scientific-priority
claim or program closure. A distinct independent-first 40-significant-bit
proposal also exists; it is not silently substituted for this 26-fractional-
grid-bit construction. Root read the complete frozen council note and its
explicit cost/exposure limits. All old screen flags, failures, grids,
thresholds and sources stay unchanged. No current Rust API or frozen sign
producer implements this magnitude recipe; no matching qualified PDF exists.

The PID remains categorical Makkeh–Gutknecht–Wibral (MGW)
[shared exclusions](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.103.032149), with
[versioned source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03356v5). This numerical kernel
does not introduce a new PID or transfer the result to continuous, angular,
hyperbolic or I_min quantities. Standard exact rational enclosure ideas are
not claimed as inventions.

## 1. Input and every starting symbol

For finite categorical sources and target, fix an ordinary MGW antichain:
a nonempty family of nonempty source-position subsets, with AND inside each
subset and OR between subsets. Joint anchors indexed by \(s\) have positive
integer counts \(c_s\); row count is \(N=\sum_s c_s>0\).
For that anchor/node, the signed component ratio is \(a_s/b_s>0\), formed
from its exact marginal and target-conditional component arguments. Both
components use marginal averaging weights \(c_s/N\), not target-pool weights.
Define positive integer products and signed average

\[
P=\prod_s a_s^{c_s},\qquad Q=\prod_s b_s^{c_s},\qquad
A=\frac1N\ln(P/Q).
\]

The [complete count/component derivation](POSITIVE_CATEGORICAL_COMPONENT_BOUNDS_2026_10_11.md)
supplies this correspondence; the numerical proof below assumes it is faithful.
It also applies to other correctly supplied positive integer \(P,Q,N\),
without asserting that arbitrary such inputs realize a categorical PID law.
Units are nats: \(\ln\) is the natural logarithm.

Exact input arithmetic assumes correct alphabets, encoding, support, alignment,
target conditioning and integer multiplicities. It assumes neither independent
sources nor independent/identically distributed rows, Gaussian data,
stationarity, population calibration or task benefit. Real/irrational weights
and floating-only probabilities are outside this count contract. A wrong
estimand is not repaired by precise evaluation.

## 2. Decide sign, then reduce without floating logarithms

Compare \(P,Q\) exactly. Equality gives \(A=0\) and interval \([0,0]\),
with no relative percentage. Otherwise retain the sign and let
\(J=\max(P,Q)\), \(K=\min(P,Q)\), \(R=J/K>1\), and
\(y=\ln R= N|A|>0\). The letters \(J,K\) denote integers, not data rows.

Exact shifted-integer comparisons choose integer \(e\ge0\) such that
\(2^eK\le J<2^{e+1}K\). Define the reduced argument
\(v=R/2^e\in[1,2)\) and

\[
d=J-2^eK,\qquad f=J+2^eK,\qquad
z=d/f=(v-1)/(v+1)\in[0,1/3).
\]

Here \(d\) is an integer difference, not dimension; \(f\) is its positive
normalizing sum. The reduced argument \(v\) is not variance or velocity.
These exact differences avoid loss from subtracting floating logarithms.
Then \(y=e\ln2+\ln v\), a sum of nonnegative terms.

Fix \(B=26\) fractional grid bits and \(\eta=2^{-B}\) before outputs.
This is not a claim of 26-significant-bit output: a normalized integer below
can have 27 significant bits and its upper neighbor can carry another bit.

## 3. Small rational logarithm kernel and complete tail

For rational \(x\in[0,1/3]\), define lower sum and tail ceiling

\[
\ell(x)=2\sum_{j=0}^{4}\frac{x^{2j+1}}{2j+1},\qquad
\tau(x)=\frac{2x^{11}}{11(1-x^2)}.
\]

The index \(j\) labels the five retained terms. For integration coordinate
\(t\in[0,x]\), the finite identity
\((1-t^2)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{4}t^{2j}+t^{10}/(1-t^2)\) gives

\[
\ln\frac{1+x}{1-x}=\ell(x)+2\int_0^x\frac{t^{10}}{1-t^2}\,dt.
\]

The remainder is nonnegative. Replacing its denominator by \(1-x^2\)
and integrating proves containment in \([\ell(x),\ell(x)+\tau(x)]\).
For \(x>0\), \(\ell(x)\ge2x\), so

\[
\tau(x)/\ell(x)\le\frac{x^{10}}{11(1-x^2)}
\le\rho,\qquad \rho=\frac1{577368}.
\]

The last bound uses \(x\le1/3\). At zero both quantities are zero;
use that branch, not a zero denominator. Differentiating the finite sum gives
\(\ell'(x)=2\sum_{j=0}^{4}x^{2j}\le2/(1-x^2)\le9/4\).
Consequently increasing \(x\) by \(h\) within the domain increases
\(\ell\) by at most \(9h/4\). This includes rounding error in the proof.

## 4. Branch I: the range exponent is positive

When \(e\ge1\), let grid step \(h=2^{-B}\), integer
\(t_0=\lfloor z/h\rfloor\), and endpoints
\(z_0=t_0h\), \(z_1=\min((t_0+1)h,1/3)\).
Floor means rounding down. The upper cap is the exact domain endpoint, not
an observed-outcome adjustment. Then \(z_0\le z\le z_1\) and
\(z_1-z_0\le h\). Define positive logarithm endpoints

\[
L=e\ell(1/3)+\ell(z_0),\qquad
U=e[\ell(1/3)+\tau(1/3)]+\ell(z_1)+\tau(z_1).
\]

Monotonicity and the preceding tail give \(L\le y\le U\) and
\(L\ge2e/3\ge2/3\). Let \(\Delta=\ell(z_1)-\ell(z_0)\).
It obeys \(0\le\Delta\le9h/4\). Applying each tail bound gives

\[
U-L\le\rho L+(1+\rho)\Delta,
\qquad \frac{U-L}{2L}\le\frac\rho2+(1+\rho)\frac{27\eta}{16}.
\]

This remains valid when \(z_0=0\): the positive \(e\ln2\) term
supplies a positive lower sum. A tiny residual is not divided by itself.

## 5. Branch II: zero exponent, moderate reduced argument

If \(e=0\), then \(R>1\) forces \(z>0\). Exact comparisons choose
integer \(m\) with \(2^m\le z<2^{m+1}\), so \(m\le-2\).
No floating log2 is required. Set
\(h=2^{m-B}\), \(t_0=\lfloor z/h\rfloor\),
\(z_0=t_0h\), \(z_1=\min((t_0+1)h,1/3)\).
Now \(t_0\ge2^B\), \(z_0>0\), and \(h/z_0\le\eta\).

Use this polynomial branch only when \(z_1>2^{-12}\). Set
\(L=\ell(z_0)\), \(U=\ell(z_1)+\tau(z_1)\).
Containment follows as before. Since \(L\ge2z_0\),

\[
\frac{U-L}{2L}\le\frac\rho2+(1+\rho)\frac{9\eta}{16}.
\]

Also \(z_1\le z_0+h\le z_0(1+\eta)<2z_0\), hence
\(z_0>2^{-13}\). Therefore \(m\ge-13\): the dyadic endpoints
have at most \(B-m\le39\) fractional bits, or the exact cap \(1/3\).
Polynomial powers use these small rationals, not the original huge denominator.

## 6. Branch III: zero exponent and tiny reduced argument

When \(e=0\) and \(z_1\le2^{-12}\), do not expand the huge scale.
Direct integration gives

\[
2z\le\ln v\le\frac{2z}{1-z^2}.
\]

Because \(z_1^2\le2^{-24}\) and
\((1-u)^{-1}\le1+2u\) for \(0\le u\le1/2\), where \(u\)
is the temporary squared argument, this implies
\(y\in[2z_0,2z_1(1+2^{-23})]\). The cap \(1/3\) is inactive.
The width divided by twice the positive lower endpoint is at most

\[
\frac{(1+\eta)(1+2^{-23})-1}{2}
<2^{-22}<\frac1{1000000}.
\]

For the first strict comparison, expand the numerator: the ceiling is
\(\eta/2+2^{-24}+\eta2^{-24}<2^{-27}+2^{-23}<2^{-22}\).
The final inequality uses \(2^{22}=4194304>1000000\).

Store common scale \(k=m-B+1\) as an exact signed integer and return

\[
\left[\frac{t_0}{N},\frac{(t_0+1)(1+2^{-23})}{N}\right]2^k.
\]

This means two exact small rational coefficients and an integer exponent,
not two flattened binary64/decimal values. Never materialize \(2^k\)
just for serialization. Reflect and reverse endpoints for a negative average.
Unreported conversion/underflow would invalidate this contract.

## 7. Uniform relative ceiling and returned signs

For both polynomial branches, \(\rho/2=1/1154736<9/10000000\).
Since \(1+\rho<2\) and \(2^{26}=67108864\),

\[
(1+\rho)27\eta/16<\frac{27}{8\cdot67108864}<\frac1{10000000}.
\]

The last exact comparison is \(270000000<536870912\).
The Branch II addend is smaller. Thus each branch has
\((U-L)/(2L)<1/1000000\). If \(C\) is an enclosing interval's
midpoint and its positive true value is \(y\ge L\), containment gives
\(|C-y|/y\le(U-L)/(2L)\). Division by exact \(N\) and sign
reflection preserve this relative bound. Exact zero remains its separate
branch without a percentage. Ppm means numerical relative error, not confidence.

This is an openly different numerical algorithm. The old absolute-grid
obstruction, cheap Taylor widths and unresolved labels remain valid evidence.
The preset branches/grid/cutoff were not tuned to dataset outcomes.

## 8. Hand tiny-value example — numerical kernel only

Take \(P=2^{100}+1\), \(Q=2^{100}\), \(N=100\). These are numerical
kernel inputs; no realization as a categorical count law is claimed.
Then \(A=\ln(1+2^{-100})/100>0\), \(e=0\), and
\(z=1/(2^{101}+1)\). Since \(2^{101}<2^{101}+1<2^{102}\),
\(m=-102\), \(h=2^{-128}\). Exact integer division gives
\(t_0=2^{27}-1\): multiplying that integer by the denominator gives
\(2^{128}-2^{101}+2^{27}-1<2^{128}\), whereas multiplying the next
integer gives \(2^{128}+2^{27}>2^{128}\).

Consequently \(z_0=(2^{27}-1)2^{-128}\), \(z_1=2^{-101}\),
so the tiny branch applies. Store exponent \(k=-127\) and coefficients
\((2^{27}-1)/100\) and \(2^{27}(1+2^{-23})/100\). Their interval
contains the stated average and meets the uniform relative bound by the
previous proof. No floating subtraction, tiny decimal, gigantic polynomial
denominator or native example run is needed. Exact shifted division still
needs enough integer bits; a wrapping u128 implementation would not be justified.

## 9. Rust implementation and computational cost

The current Rust API and sign-only producer do not implement this recipe.
A new typed research companion would need checked arbitrary integers,
bounded parsing/product plans, exact outward division, coefficient/scale
serialization, cancellation/resource refusal and implementation refinement.
The [Rust cost baseline](../RUST_IMPLEMENTATION_AND_COST.md) stays unchanged.

Let \(H=\max(\operatorname{bits}(P),\operatorname{bits}(Q))\), where
bits counts binary digits. Range reduction, integer differences and scaled
division use operands of at most \(H+B+3\) bits. To see the relative
branch bound, numerator and denominator leading bits first determine a
candidate \(m\); one exact shifted comparison confirms it. Scaling a tiny
numerator by \(2^{B-m}\) aligns it with the denominator plus the fixed
grid bits, rather than raising the whole denominator to degree eleven.
The number of such large operations is fixed after \(P,Q\) are formed;
their time, memory and division workspace remain nonconstant in \(H\).
This operand bound covers argument extraction, not every output operation.
Endpoint coefficients also depend on the binary digit counts of \(N\)
and \(e\); the count-cap calculation below includes them. Arbitrarily large
\(N\) cannot be omitted from a general serialization budget.

For each small kernel argument write \(x=a/b\), positive integer \(b\),
\(0\le a/b\le1/3\), with \(b\le2^{39}\) or \(b=3\).
Use the direct common denominator \(315b^9\) for \(\ell\), since
315 is the least common multiple of 1,3,5,7,9. Use
\(11b^9(b^2-a^2)\) for \(\tau\). The lower-sum numerator is
\(2\sum_{j=0}^{4}[315/(2j+1)]a^{2j+1}b^{8-2j}\).
Since \(a\le b\), it is at most \(3150b^9<2^{363}\);
its denominator is below \(2^{360}\). The tail numerator is
\(2a^{11}\le2b^{11}\le2^{430}\), and its positive denominator
is at most \(11b^{11}<2^{433}\). Thus each base numerator/denominator
is below \(2^{512}\). Do not multiply an
equivalent fraction by arbitrary common factors and retain this cost claim.

Under three sources, \(N\le M=31000\) and direct count-product
representatives/reductions give \(H\le120N+1\le3720001\).
Then \(e\le H<2^{22}\), \(N<2^{15}\). Combining at most four
specified base terms gives endpoint numerator below \(2^{2072}\)
and denominator below \(2^{2063}\): four denominator factors cost at most
2048 bits, multiplication by \(e\) adds 22, summing four adds two,
and division by \(N\) adds 15 to the denominator. A 4,096-bit coefficient
ceiling is therefore deliberately conservative. Greatest-common-divisor
reduction can shrink it. Tiny-branch coefficients are smaller, while their
exact scale remains a separate integer.

This count bound is not a measured memory, deadline or output-size result.
Constructing \(P,Q\), carrier enumeration and reading earlier records still
cost separately. No existing 180-second, 256-MiB or 8-MiB admission transfers.
For unbounded counts, coefficient/exponent/input budgets must be separately
declared. Missing, overflowed, timed-out or out-of-budget work must abstain.

## 11. Distinct 40-significant-bit outward-output alternative

The independently fixed alternative has compact dyadic output rather than
the preceding raw rational coefficients. Its constants and bound are distinct;
do not mix the recipes. Set significant precision \(p=40\), relative rounding
allowance \(u=2^{-39}\), absolute argument grid \(G=2^{40}\), and tiny
cutoff \(2^{-10}\). The five-term \(\ell,\tau,\rho\) are as above.
Here \(p\) denotes precision, not a probability or component value.

For positive rational \(x\), exact comparisons choose integer \(j\) with
\(2^j\le x<2^{j+1}\). Put \(r=j-39\) and define outward dyadics

\[
D_-(x)=\lfloor x2^{-r}\rfloor2^r,\qquad
D_+(x)=\lceil x2^{-r}\rceil2^r.
\]

Ceiling rounds up. The retained integer coefficient is at least \(2^{39}\).
If the upper coefficient equals \(2^{40}\), replace it by \(2^{39}\)
and add one to the exponent; the represented value is unchanged. Hence each
nonzero coefficient has 40 magnitude bits. A sign is separate, or the storage
type needs at least 41 signed bits; a signed 40-bit integer is insufficient.
Since the grid step is at most \(ux\),

\[
(1-u)x\le D_-(x)\le x\le D_+(x)\le(1+u)x.
\]

At zero use exact zero. Exponents stay exact integers, not expanded powers.

If \(e=0\) and \(0<z<2^{-10}\), define
\(\beta=1/[3(2^{20}-1)]<2^{-21}\). The finite one-term remainder gives
\(2z\le\ln v\le2z[1+z^2/(3(1-z^2))]\le2z(1+\beta)\).
Thus pre-output magnitude endpoints are

\[
l=2D_-(z)/N,\qquad t=2(1+\beta)D_+(z)/N.
\]

Here \(l,t\) are lower/upper magnitude endpoints, not target labels.
Their relative width obeys
\(t/l-1\le[\beta+2u+u\beta]/(1-u)<2^{-19}\):
the numerator is below \(2^{-21}+3u<2^{-20}\), and
\(1-u>1/2\). Only small coefficients and symbolic scale are formed.
The tail uses the actual \(z\) cutoff, even if its outward upper neighbor
slightly exceeds that cutoff.

Otherwise \(e\ge1\) or \(z\ge2^{-10}\). Use
\(z_0=\lfloor Gz\rfloor/G\),
\(z_1=\min(1/3,\lceil Gz\rceil/G)\), and the Branch I formulas for
\(L,U\), now with these 40-bit endpoints. They enclose \(y\).
If \(e\ge1\), \(L\ge2/3\). If \(e=0\),
\(z_0\ge2^{-10}-2^{-40}>2^{-11}\), so \(L>2^{-10}\).
The change in \(\ell\) is at most \((9/4)2^{-40}\); therefore

\[
(U-L)/L<\Lambda,
\qquad \Lambda=2^{-19}+2^{-27}.
\]

Indeed \(\rho<2^{-19}\), and the rounding addend is below
\(2(9/4)2^{-30}<2^{-27}\). Set \(l=L/N,t=U/N\).
Both branches have \(t/l-1<\Lambda\). Normalize the final endpoints
outward once more: \(l_c=D_-(l)\), \(t_c=D_+(t)\).
With exact midpoint \(C_{\mathrm{mag}}\), their relative ceiling is

\[
\frac{t_c-l_c}{2l_c}
\le\frac{\Lambda+2u+u\Lambda}{2(1-u)}
<2^{-19}+2^{-25}<2^{-18}
=\frac1{262144}<\frac1{250000}.
\]

The numerator bound comes from \(t-l\le\Lambda l\) and outward
rounding errors at most \(ut\) and \(ul\); the denominator is at least
\(2(1-u)l\). For the strict comparison, \(\Lambda<1\),
\(u<2^{-27}\) and \(2(1-u)>1\) give an upper bound
\(\Lambda+3u<2^{-19}+4\cdot2^{-27}\).
Reflect/reverse for a negative average; equality of \(P,Q\) alone gives
exact zero. This alternative meets four ppm, without a magnitude gap.

For the realizable two-copy-row law in the
[raw report](CANCELLATION_FREE_SIGNED_AVERAGE_2026_10_11.md),
\(P=4,Q=1,N=2,A=\ln2,e=2,z=0\). The normal branch has pre-output
endpoints \(4297606/6200145\) and that lower value plus \(1/866052\).
The formulas for \(D_-,D_+\) specify their exact compact outward output;
these are hand numbers, not an executed new fixture.

Cost is not just a function of product size. Let \(\nu=\operatorname{bits}(N)\)
and \(E=\max(1,\operatorname{bits}(e))\). Argument extraction/quantization
uses at most two large divisions on operands bounded by \(H+41\) bits.
Final endpoint normalization uses additional divisions bounded by
\(E+\nu+1024\) bits for the normal branch, or \(\nu+128\) for the
tiny branch. Arbitrarily large \(N\) cannot be omitted from this accounting.

For small arguments \(a/b\) with \(b\le2^{40}\), the same specified
common forms bound \(\ell\)'s numerator/denominator below
\(2^{372},2^{369}\), and \(\tau\)'s by \(2^{441},2^{444}\),
respectively. Adding them gives bounds \(2^{817},2^{813}\);
the corresponding constant at \(1/3\) is below \(2^{50},2^{46}\).
Combining \(e\) and dividing by \(N\) gives numerator below
\(2^{E+864}\), denominator below \(2^{\nu+859}\). Normalization's
extra shifts/divisions are covered by \(E+\nu+1024\).
Under the stated count cap, \(E\le22,\nu\le15\), so these operands
are at most 1,061 bits; product extraction still reaches millions of bits.
Exact exponent magnitude has the conservative ceiling \(H+\nu+1024\).
None of these hand arithmetic counts establishes runtime or memory admission.
