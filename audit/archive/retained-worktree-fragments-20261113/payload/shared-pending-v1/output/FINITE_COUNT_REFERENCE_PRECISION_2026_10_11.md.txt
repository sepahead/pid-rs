# A uniform precision bound for finite-count components

Sepehr Mahmoudian · 11 October 2026

## What this resolves

Under the exact finite-count assumptions below, the midpoint of the existing
rational logarithm-reference interval approximates every strictly positive
categorical shared-exclusions component with relative error below \(2^{-84}\).
The same bound holds for its strictly positive count-weighted average.
This is much tighter than four parts per million.

This is a hand-derived, source-reviewed numerical guarantee, not a newly
kernel-verified theorem, scientific-priority claim or population error bound.
It resolves the analytic precision question for this bounded reference
calculation. Formal admission, implementation refinement and application value
remain separate open obligations. No research program is declared closed here.

It does **not** rescue the [original cheap-bracket screen](EMPIRICAL_COMPONENT_BOUND_SCREEN_2026_10_11.md).
That screen's midpoint is different: none of its 184 positive averaged
components meets four ppm. All original flags, inputs, wide cases and criteria
remain unchanged. The reference midpoint is an explicitly different, more
expensive numerical choice, using intervals already produced by that run.

The defining measure is Makkeh–Gutknecht–Wibral (MGW) categorical shared
exclusions. The [2021 paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.103.032149)
defines the measure; the [versioned source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03356v5)
and [repository component derivation](POSITIVE_CATEGORICAL_COMPONENT_BOUNDS_2026_10_11.md)
fix the event, localization and nonnegative-component identity used here.
This is not I_min, Ehrlich's continuous functional, or a new PID definition.

## Assumptions and variables

There are exactly three categorical sources \(X_1,X_2,X_3\) and a categorical
target \(T\). Their alphabets are finite; no Gaussian, angular, continuous,
observed-source independence, independent-and-identically-distributed rows
(IID) or stationarity assumption is needed.
An occupied joint anchor \(s=(x_1,x_2,x_3,t)\) has positive integer count
\(c_s\) in a law containing \(N\) rows. Lowercase entries are fixed labels.
The empirical probabilities are these counts divided by their total.

The maximum admitted row count is \(M=31000\), and \(1\le N\le M\).
For one component the probability law \(Q\) is either the source marginal
or the source law conditional on the anchor's target label \(t\).
Its count denominator is \(D\): \(D=N\) for the marginal and \(D=C_t\)
for the conditional law, where \(C_t\) counts rows with target \(t\).
Thus \(1\le D\le N\le M\).

Fix an ordinary antichain: a nonempty family of nonempty source collections
with no collection contained in another. Its agreement event \(E\) joins
collections by OR and positions inside each collection by AND. Set
\(u=Q(E)>0\). Let \(m_1,\ldots,m_k\) be the maximal excluded agreement
patterns; \(1\le k\le3\). Set \(a_i=Q(B=m_i)\), where \(B\) is the
set of source positions agreeing with the anchor. These are exact equality-cell
masses, not masses of all subpatterns. Event and cells are disjoint, so

\[
u+\sum_{i=1}^k a_i\le1.
\]

All nonzero masses are integer multiples of \(1/D\). Positive occupied anchors
guarantee \(u\ge1/D\). We consider either nonnegative informative or
misinformative component \(p\), measured in nats. Its signed difference is
a different quantity. The existing positive integral and log-product identities
are the analytic dependencies; neither is newly kernel-verified here.

The reference uses exactly 64 rational series terms, power-of-two range
reduction and outward integer rounding onto the grid of step \(b=2^{-128}\).
All subsequent midpoint and averaging operations are exact rational operations.
There is no new binary64 rounding of endpoints or averages.

## Step 1: a positive component cannot be arbitrarily small

The positive box-integral identity is

\[
p=(k-1)!\int_0^{a_1}\cdots\int_0^{a_k}
\frac{d\eta_1\cdots d\eta_k}{(u+\eta_1+\cdots+\eta_k)^k}.
\]

The integration variables \(\eta_i\) range over cell-mass intervals; they are
not sensor readings. The exclamation mark denotes factorial, with \(0!=1\).
If any \(a_i=0\), then \(p=0\) exactly. Conversely all \(a_i>0\) give \(p>0\).

For \(p>0\), every \(a_i\ge1/D\). Throughout the box,
\(u+\sum_i\eta_i\le u+\sum_i a_i\le1\), so the integrand is at least one.
The box volume is \(\prod_i a_i\). Therefore

\[
p\ge (k-1)!\prod_i a_i\ge \frac{(k-1)!}{D^k}
\ge \frac{(k-1)!}{M^k}.
\]

For \(k=1,2,3\) these last lower bounds are \(1/M,1/M^2,2/M^3\).
Since \(M\ge2\), all are at least

\[
\delta=\frac{2}{M^3}>0.
\]

This gap comes from exact finite counts and at most three maximal excluded
cells. Arbitrary real probability masses have no such uniform gap.

## Step 2: the logarithm exponent is bounded

Set \(r_i=a_i/u\) and \(S=\sum_i r_i\). The exact component is \(p=\ln R\).
For one, two and three maximal excluded cells, respectively,

\[
R_1=1+r_1,\qquad
R_2=\frac{(1+r_1)(1+r_2)}{1+r_1+r_2},
\]

\[
R_3=
\frac{(1+r_1)(1+r_2)(1+r_3)(1+S)}
{(1+r_1+r_2)(1+r_1+r_3)(1+r_2+r_3)}.
\]

Subscripts indicate the case \(k\), not source or target labels. In case two,
each numerator factor is at most \(1+S\), giving \(R_2\le1+S\).
In case three, match the denominator factors to \(1+r_1\), \(1+r_3\)
and \(1+r_2\), respectively; each dominates its matched factor.
Thus \(R_3\le1+S\). Case one has equality. Consequently

\[
1\le R\le1+S=\frac{u+\sum_i a_i}{u}\le\frac1u\le D\le M.
\]

For \(p>0\), \(R>1\). Write \(R=2^e h\), where \(e\) is a nonnegative
integer and \(1\le h<2\). Exact comparisons determine this decomposition.
Because \(M=31000<32768=2^{15}\), \(e\le14\).

## Step 3: the 64-term raw interval is narrower than one grid step

For an argument \(v\in[1,2]\), define \(z=(v-1)/(v+1)\in[0,1/3]\).
The letter \(v\) means the range-reduced logarithm argument, not variance
or velocity. The geometric-series identity gives

\[
\ln v=2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}.
\]

Here \(j\) is a nonnegative integer term index. Keep \(j=0,\ldots,63\).
Every omitted term is nonnegative; its denominator is at least 129.
Summing the resulting geometric majorant bounds the omitted tail by

\[
\tau(z)=\frac{2z^{129}}{129(1-z^2)}
\le \frac{1}{172\,3^{128}}.
\]

The symbol \(\tau\) denotes an upper bound on the omitted logarithm tail.
Use this for both \(v=h\) and \(v=2\), and combine via
\(\ln R=e\ln2+\ln h\). If \(w_{\rm raw}\) denotes the resulting raw
interval width, then

\[
w_{\rm raw}\le\frac{e+1}{172\,3^{128}}
\le\frac{15}{172\,3^{128}}<2^{-128}=b.
\]

The strict inequality follows from
\((15/172)(2/3)^{128}<1\); it needs no floating logarithm or chosen data.

## Step 4: outward grid rounding gives at most two grid steps

Let \(l\) and \(h_{\rm raw}\) be the raw rational lower and upper endpoints.
The latter is an endpoint, not the earlier range-reduced argument \(h\).
The reference endpoints are

\[
L_{\rm ref}=b\lfloor l/b\rfloor,\qquad
U_{\rm ref}=b\lceil h_{\rm raw}/b\rceil .
\]

Floor and ceiling mean rounding to the next integer down and up. Put
\(n=\lfloor l/b\rfloor\), so \(l/b<n+1\).
Since \(h_{\rm raw}-l<b\), \(h_{\rm raw}/b<n+2\).
Hence \(\lceil h_{\rm raw}/b\rceil\le n+2\), and

\[
0\le U_{\rm ref}-L_{\rm ref}\le2b,\qquad
L_{\rm ref}\le p\le U_{\rm ref}.
\]

It follows that \(L_{\rm ref}\ge p-2b\ge\delta-2b>0\).
This argument uses the grid's integer structure: blindly adding two rounding
errors would give a looser three-step bound. For \(p=0\), the unchanged
reference returns exactly \([0,0]\), not a widened positive interval.

## Step 5: the local relative guarantee

Define the reference midpoint
\(C_{\rm ref}=(L_{\rm ref}+U_{\rm ref})/2\).
Then \(|C_{\rm ref}-p|\le(U_{\rm ref}-L_{\rm ref})/2\le b\).
Because the lower endpoint is positive,

\[
\frac{|C_{\rm ref}-p|}{p}
\le \frac{U_{\rm ref}-L_{\rm ref}}{2L_{\rm ref}}
\le\frac{b}{\delta-2b}
=\frac{M^3}{2(2^{128}-M^3)}<2^{-84}.
\]

The final comparison is equivalent to
\(M^3(2^{84}+2)<2^{129}\). To check it with small integers,
\(M^3=29791000000000\), and

\[
8M^3=238328000000000
<246290604621824=7\cdot2^{45}.
\]

Also \(2^{84}+2<(8/7)2^{84}\), because \(14<2^{84}\).
Multiplying these two strict inequalities yields the required comparison.
This is hand-checkable exact arithmetic, not a fresh solver/kernel receipt.

## Step 6: averaging does not lose precision to tiny anchor weights

Fix a component position and side. For every occupied anchor \(s\), let
\(w_s=c_s/N>0\), \(p_s\) its component and
\([L_{{\rm ref},s},U_{{\rm ref},s}]\) its reference interval.
The weights sum to one. Define the averaged component and endpoints by

\[
\bar p=\sum_s w_s p_s,\qquad
\bar L_{\rm ref}=\sum_s w_s L_{{\rm ref},s},\qquad
\bar U_{\rm ref}=\sum_s w_s U_{{\rm ref},s}.
\]

Bars mean count-weighted averaging within one law, not pooling datasets.
Let \(\mathcal P=\{s:p_s>0\}\) and
\(W=\sum_{s\in\mathcal P}w_s\), the total weight of positive local components.
If \(\bar p>0\), nonnegativity implies \(W>0\).
Zero components contribute exactly zero endpoints. Thus

\[
\bar L_{\rm ref}\ge W(\delta-2b),\qquad
\bar U_{\rm ref}-\bar L_{\rm ref}\le2bW.
\]

The same factor \(W\) cancels in the relative ceiling:

\[
\frac{\bar U_{\rm ref}-\bar L_{\rm ref}}{2\bar L_{\rm ref}}
\le\frac{2bW}{2W(\delta-2b)}
=\frac{b}{\delta-2b}<2^{-84}.
\]

This is stronger than separately using \(\bar p\ge\delta/N\) and a
weight-independent width bound. That cruder route unnecessarily loses a
factor \(N\). The cancellation requires exact weighting, nonnegative
components and exact zero references; extra rounding needs a new analysis.

## Signed quantities, negative routes and scope limits

The informative and misinformative components can nearly cancel. Their
reference intervals give a signed net interval by cross-subtraction.
Each component width is at most \(2b\), including averages, so a net interval
has width at most \(4b\); its midpoint has absolute error at most \(2b\) nats.
This does not give the same uniform relative bound for the net.
At an exact zero the relative ratio is undefined. A nonzero local net has
additional finite-rational structure that could supply a different, weaker
gap; that is not the present screen or the argument above.

For a constant target, informative and misinformative components can be equal
and positive while their signed difference is zero. Reinterpreting positive
component precision as relative net precision would therefore be wrong.
Sparse empirical zeros do not establish population support or zero dependence.

Other rejected transfers: arbitrary real masses with no positive-count gap;
unbounded \(N\); more than three sources with the same \(\delta\); a reference
with fewer terms or different precision; rounding the exact averages into
binary64 without an outward-error analysis; generated forecasts used as real
outcomes; approximation intervals treated as sampling confidence intervals.
For a concrete out-of-contract failure, take a binary two-state source law
and a constant target: the anchor has full-agreement count \(D-1\), and the
other state matches none of its three source labels, with count one.
At the bottom antichain, \(k=1\) and \(p=\ln(D/(D-1))>0\).
For \(D=2^{131}\), the inequality \(\ln(1+x)<x\), \(x>0\), gives
\(p<2/D=b/4\). The raw upper reference endpoint is at most
\(p+1/(172\,3^{128})<b\);
its positive lower endpoint is also below \(b\). Outward grid rounding gives
\([0,b]\), whose midpoint has relative error greater than one. This is a
hand-derived realizable example outside the count cap, not an executed control
or a failure of the admitted helper, which refuses that pool size.

The same formula could be generalized with new explicit count/dimension/tail
conditions, but this document does not silently grant those extensions.
Splitting, downsampling or refitting a larger law merely to enter the cap
changes the empirical object; it is not an invisible precision repair.

## Rust implementation and computational cost

The stable categorical discrete_sxpid3 API implements MGW empirical atoms,
without an experimental feature. It does not expose this exact-rational
reference-midpoint certificate. The current reference is in the research
helper preserved as [inert source text](data/component-bound-screen-20261011/screen_midpoint_bounds.py.txt).
Neither a Rust interval implementation nor formal Rust refinement was run.
The [implementation baseline](../RUST_IMPLEMENTATION_AND_COST.md) retains
the broader API/resource boundaries.

Inputs are finite count laws over three sources and a target, \(N\le31000\).
The observed occupancy screen uses four labels per source and two/four target
labels; the proof itself does not require those alphabet sizes. It needs
exact occupied counts, anchor agreement events and \(k\le3\).
No preprocessing, fitting, inference, resampling or row-independence repair
is added. Offline numerical evaluation is intended; real-time suitability
and prediction benefit remain unmeasured.

Given masses, the cheap bracket uses order-\(k\) rational operations. The
reference instead forms up to \(2^k\) factors, range-reduces an exact rational,
uses 64 terms and an explicit tail, then floor/ceil and exact weighted sums.
Large rational numerators/denominators and greatest-common-divisor work have
nonconstant bit costs. Fixed term count is not constant elapsed time.
Finite carrier, extraction, parser, resource and serialization costs remain.

No new run or timing is claimed for this theorem. The existing screen's
whole-route measurements and refusal limits remain [reported separately](EMPIRICAL_COMPONENT_BOUND_SCREEN_2026_10_11.md#rust-implementation-and-computational-cost).
A future approximation API needs explicit cancellation, allocation and
resource contracts; finite formulas do not supply them. The unchanged helper's
cooperative checks are not hard memory quotas or universal signal handling.

## Application meaning and closure status

The real occupancy count laws already satisfy the stated count cap. Their
existing reference intervals can therefore support a conditional near-exact
component/average approximation even when the original cheap bracket is wide.
This answers a numerical reliability question on those empirical laws.
It does not prove that quantization is appropriate, that sensors should be
removed, that a predictor improves or that population uncertainty is small.

A defensible future diagnostic uses the cheap bracket when its declared error
is acceptable, otherwise the separately identified rational reference, or
abstains if resource/assumption checks fail. This is a proposed selection
contract, not a modified executed benchmark. Simple direct prediction and
task-loss baselines remain necessary for application claims.

Two initial mathematical council conclusions preceded exchange of fresh
proposals or new numerical outputs. One reviewer authored the original helper;
the other authored related exposition, not that helper. Both share earlier
context/model/host/formulas. Root checked the argument itself; advisory
agreement is not proof, human verification or kernel acceptance.

Analytic bounded precision: hand-derived under explicit dependencies.
Executable instance arithmetic: the unchanged screen exists.
New formal theorem, binary64/Rust refinement, statistical calibration,
matched application benefit and qualified matching PDF: OPEN.
SxPID3 A–E, original finite formal obligations, angular, hyperbolic and v1
are not closed by this result. Negative evidence and all original screen
criteria remain unchanged.
