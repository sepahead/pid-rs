# Retained counterexample: atom-to-joint scale does not guarantee acceptance

## False statement

The proposed sufficient condition

```text
max(|U1|, |U2|, |Syn|) <= 16 * |J|
```

does not imply that `Pid2Result::from_estimate` accepts, even when all four inputs and all four
candidate atoms are finite and both unique-coordinate reconstructions pass. The inequality is an
exact-real comparison here; it is not evaluated by overflowing binary64 multiplication.

## Exact raw-bit counterexample

```text
I1 = 0x7fe44dcfb32f7b9b
I2 = 0x7fbf796113915533
J  = 0x7fefffffffffffff
R  = 0x7fd1e3b3adc820e6

U1  = 0x7fd6b7ebb896d650
U2  = 0xffc40ab6d1c79732
Syn = 0x7fe0b4de01426a31
```

The candidate atoms are the correctly rounded values of their individual represented-input
forms. Every atom magnitude is at most `f64::MAX`, hence strictly below the exact real number
`16 * |J|`. The two unique-coordinate reconstructions have ordered distances zero and one from
their supplied inputs. Exact accumulation of `[R,U1,U2,Syn]`, however, rounds to

```text
0x7ff0000000000000  (+infinity),
```

so the finite/identity guard rejects.

## Independent checks and dependency reach

The witness was derived on the integer `2^-1074` grid and independently replayed with the
repository's `Fraction` decoder and integer round-to-nearest-even routine. It falsifies only the
proposed sufficient condition and any wording that ordinary atom-to-joint scale alone guarantees
acceptance. It does not falsify the exact candidate-synergy contract, the acceptance-subset proof,
or the unchanged fail-closed guard.

Any repaired sufficient theorem needs at least an explicit overflow-margin premise and a complete
ordered-distance proof across binade changes, subnormals, signed zero, and sign crossings. No such
theorem is claimed by revision 3.
