# Candidate finite-encoder transfer and practical decision

## The practical missing score

The complete-key score s(z)=p'_theta(z)/p_theta(z) may be unavailable when a simulator or encoder can sample categorical outputs but cannot enumerate their marginal law. Requiring that marginal score would make the preceding stopped-gradient result less useful. A latent-score transfer can address this gap under explicit assumptions; it does not estimate a score from the same data without analysis.

Let X have a finite law w(x) independent of theta, and let C be drawn from a differentiable conditional PMF q_theta(c|x) with a fixed positive support. Let the complete key Z=f(X,C) be a fixed finite categorical map. The target Y is one fixed component of f. The latent law is P_theta(x,c)=w(x)q_theta(c|x), and its accessible scalar score is

    ell(x,c) = partial_theta log q_theta(c|x).

On its fixed positive support, normalization gives E ell=0. Suppose |ell|<=L. The image mass is p_theta(z)=sum_{f(x,c)=z}P_theta(x,c), with a fixed positive support. Its marginal score obeys

    p'_theta(z)/p_theta(z)=E[ell(X,C)|Z=z].

This is a finite conditional-mean identity, derived by differentiating that finite sum. It is not the claim ell(X,C)=s(Z) pointwise.

Draw IID latent rows (X_i,C_i), compute native increments only from image keys Z_i, and stop at the first repeated image key Z_tau=Z_0. Use the complete latent prefix score sum in each causal term. Each finite prefix expectation differentiates with its latent product score. The killing argument is unchanged because the native increments depend only on image keys. Either finite-latent dominated differentiation or the explicit image conditional-score identity should identify the stopped expectation with the image MGW atom derivative. These are proof obligations, not accepted transfer credit.

The prospective support-size variance proof can also use the latent score. Conditional on an anchor image z, the initial score is now random but bounded by L and independent of future rows. Its L2 term is still <=L H_n. Future raw failure rows and target cycles remain IID with the required conditional score means. For a raw success row, the mean is s(z)=E[ell|Z=z]. For a complete target cycle ending at target y, the success mean is s(z)-a_y, where a_y=E[ell|Y=y]. The failure-cycle mean is E[ell|Y=y,Z!=z]-a_y. These are conditional means, not the realized anchor score; subtracting a_y is essential when the target marginal varies. The same mean-zero and second-moment calculations therefore appear to give 117 K L^2, where K counts image keys rather than latent states. This needs an explicit reviewed theorem before use. In particular, two latent rows with equal image keys can have different scores; never replace the terminal latent score by the anchor latent score pathwise.

## A concrete empirical training experiment

Take a finite table of synchronized two-camera records indexed by U, with fixed positive weights, features x_1(U), x_2(U), and an externally specified categorical label y(U). Use the complete record index as the finite base X, so equal feature pairs can have different labels. The PID repeat key is (C_1,C_2,y(U)); it excludes U. Each encoder sees only its declared camera features. Draw its codes independently conditional on U, so the joint conditional law is the product of the two Bernoulli laws. Example targets are a held-out task state or a separately defined action label. The latter is offline action prediction, not successful closed-loop control. Let each camera encoder produce one binary code C_i using a logistic conditional distribution with finite logits. Conditional independent encoder draws are a model choice; they do not assert that camera observations are physically independent. The MGW source variables are C_1 and C_2, and the target is Y.

For a finite training table sampled independently with replacement using the fixed weights, w is the declared empirical row law. All four joint code words have positive probability at each supported record; positive marginal code probabilities alone would not establish this. A positive stochastic encoder and a fixed observed target support give K=4|supp(Y)| complete code/target keys. For a binary target K=8, so the proposed stopping experiment draws nine latent rows on average, counting anchor and terminal repeat. With three binary sources and a binary target K=16; with four sources K=32. The lattice/update cost and score evaluation are additional costs. These expected counts do not bound latency or memory in an actual implementation.

For one logistic encoder with fixed bounded feature vector phi(x), its vector score is (C-q_theta(1|x))phi(x). Thus its norm is at most ||phi(x)||. A scalar coordinate affecting one encoder has the corresponding coordinate bound. If a scalar parameter affects both encoders, use the sum of both log-probability derivatives and bound that complete score. The current proved-in-prose bound is scalar; a Hilbert-space extension looks compatible with the same norm inequalities but requires its own statement. Deep-network parameter scores need their own justified bound. Clipping a score changes the derivative estimator and cannot be used silently to satisfy L.

Fixed features can come from images, audio, thermal measurements or geometric embeddings. The result concerns the specified categorical code law after that transform. It does not become an Ehrlich continuous or hyperbolic PID theorem. A high-dimensional feature input does not itself enlarge K, but it can affect encoder cost, score bounds, approximation quality and the meaning of the coding. Increasing code alphabet sizes grows K multiplicatively; the result does not remove that support-size cost.

IID resampling from a fixed recorded table is valid for this empirical objective. It does not prove that physical frames are IID, that unseen contexts are represented, that population gradients are estimated without error, or that a model generalizes. Training, validation and test splits must respect episodes and leakage. Estimating an additional unknown environmental law introduces a separate statistical problem.

## Why not optimize an arbitrary PID atom

A correct gradient does not make its objective useful. The fixed-world equal-synergy counterexample remains a reason not to treat synergy as a universal loss-robustness score. For two sources and independent availability masks with weights w_0,w_1,w_2,w_12, the Bayes log-loss identity is

    expected Bayes loss = H(Y)
      - w_1 I(C_1;Y) - w_2 I(C_2;Y) - w_12 I(C_1,C_2;Y).

The mask probabilities sum to one and the empty mask supplies no source information. For optimization equivalence, these weights and the target marginal must be parameter independent; the fixed-label table construction meets the latter condition. The identity assumes the mask is independent of the complete data law and predictors attain the conditional Bayes distribution under log loss. Informative missingness, finite trained models or another task loss require a different analysis.

For any consistent two-source decomposition with atoms R,U_1,U_2,S, the information term is exactly

    (w_1+w_2+w_12)R + (w_1+w_12)U_1
      + (w_2+w_12)U_2 + w_12 S.

This is a rewriting of weighted mutual information, not a PID-specific optimization advantage. Signed MGW atoms are retained; no nonnegativity is assumed. Use direct task loss, weighted MI, masked-model training and ablation as primary comparators. A standalone PID objective needs a separate justified scientific question about the allocation of information, and a benchmark showing what that constraint or diagnostic adds.

The candidate estimator could be useful when such an MGW-specific question is already justified and marginal image probabilities are expensive to enumerate, while conditional encoder scores are available. Direct differentiation of the explicitly summed image PMF is the simplest exact comparator when feasible. Common-random-number finite differences, the existing bounded-horizon score estimator with its actual bias bound, and the whole-output stopped estimator are other comparators. No speed, variance dominance across all laws, learning benefit, or robotics value is claimed without experiments and formal correspondence.

## Retained correction witness

Let image masses be p_t(A)=p_t(B)=(1+t)/4 and p_t(C)=(1-t)/2 for |t|<1, with target 0 for A,B and target 1 for C. At t=0, scores are (1,1,-1). For anchor A, a_0=1 and s(A)=1. A target-0 cycle has M copies of C followed by A or B; M has geometric-failure probability 1/2 and mean 1. Its score is 1-M, whose success and failure conditional means are both 0. The predecessor's uncentered success mean would give 1 and is false. The repaired mean s(A)-a_0=0 satisfies the mean-zero mixture relation. The 117 K L^2 argument requires these centered means; this witness does not refute the repaired bound.
