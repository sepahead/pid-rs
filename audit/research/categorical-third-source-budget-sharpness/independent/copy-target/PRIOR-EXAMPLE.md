## The two published calculations

Finn and Lizier studied a target bit that equals the first source bit. The second source bit is occasionally wrong. Each of the two matching pairs has probability 3/8. Each mismatched pair has probability 1/8. Their specificity and ambiguity decomposition gives the noisy source a mean unique contribution of −0.811 bit. A bit here is an information unit calculated with a base-two logarithm. [Finn and Lizier, §5.4 and Figure 6](https://doi.org/10.3390/e20040297)

Makkeh, Gutknecht and Wibral explicitly credit this distribution to Finn and Lizier. They calculate the categorical shared-exclusions decomposition on it. Their Table III prints −0.367 bit for the noisy source alone and +0.367 bit for the contribution available only from the pair. The opposite signs, the equal magnitudes and the copied target were already present in that paper. [Makkeh, Gutknecht and Wibral, revision 5, §VI.C and Table III, p. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.03356v5#page=11)

## Recompute the ratios at a mismatched cell

At either mismatched cell the two marginal probabilities are both 1/2 and the cell probability is 1/8. The probability that either source matches its reference value is 7/8. The first source matches whenever the target is fixed to the observed bit. Thus the signed shared term is the base-two logarithm of 8/7. The information from the noisy source alone is the logarithm of 1/2. Subtracting the shared term leaves the logarithm of 7/16 at the noisy-source unique position. The joint-only signed term is the logarithm of its reciprocal, 16/7.

The two component channels give a separate check on this last entry. In the informative channel the full pair contributes the logarithm of 8, before subtraction. Below that position the shared part is the logarithm of 8/7 and both unique parts are the logarithm of 7/4. The joint-only informative remainder is the logarithm of 16/7. In the misinformative channel the full pair and the noisy source alone each contribute 2 bits before subtraction. Both the shared term and the first source term are zero. The joint-only misinformative remainder is zero.

This is a source comparison by the project. The caption on the cited revision of Table III prints the logarithm of 16/17 for its symbol *f* in the informative joint-only column. The 16/7 factor above comes from the stated probabilities and definitions. The project has no publisher confirmation of a correction. Its existing two-source Rust regression checks the 16/7 ratio and the signed noisy-source mean on 32 observations that realize this distribution. The unchanged test passed on source revision 6a9f087b. Its checked endpoint is the two-source Rust result on these counts. When the residual is constant, the target is a copy of the first source. Inserting a constant middle source places the two-source atoms at four of the 18 three-source positions. With these source and target roles, the five mean signed contributions associated with adding the third source always sum to zero. They all vanish individually exactly when each occupied cell of the first and third sources stands alone in its row or its column. A cell sharing both margins gives two nonzero opposite means. The other three are zero. At any rule the signed cumulative is the sum of signed atoms at or below that rule in the order.

The project exhibits two four-observation tables with the same values of all 16 Shannon subset entropies. Only one table has nonzero cancellation in those five means. Its separate robot application checks the criterion on four counts from 117 real wrist records and tests both implementations on the resulting empirical distribution. ## Why the middle source must stay constant

Let the first source and the third source be independent fair bits. Give each of the four pairs one observation and let the target copy the first bit. With a constant middle source, each occupied cell has probability 1/4. Both marginals are 1/2, so the row-or-column match probability is 3/4. The five signed means are minus the natural logarithm of 4/3 for the third source alone, plus the same amount for sources one and three together, and three zeros.

Now set the middle source equal to the third bit. All four first–third pairs still occur, but every selected mean is zero. Duplicating a source gives both indices the same match event and changes the atom positions in the three-source order. The original support checker already tested these two four-row inputs. Its exponentials of four times the sum of absolute means are 65536/6561 and one. This example distinguishes the two source designs. The wrist analysis uses the constant middle source.
