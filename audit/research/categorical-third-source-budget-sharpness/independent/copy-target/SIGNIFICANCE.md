# When does a shared-exclusions calculation add information?

## Three categorical sources

A categorical reading takes a value from a finite set. Let the readings be $S_1,S_2,S_3$, and let $T$ be the target. Use the empirical distribution that assigns probability $c/N$ to a joint tuple seen $c$ times in $N$ observations. A *signed mean atom* is one contribution to the finite shared-exclusions decomposition, averaged over this distribution. Let $M$ be the sum of the absolute values of the five such contributions for which every alternative source group contains $S_3$. Write $H$ for Shannon entropy, use natural logarithms, and put $A=H(S_3\mid S_1,S_2)$, $B=H(S_3\mid S_1,S_2,T)$, and $G=A+B-M$. The order of averaging and taking an absolute value matters. [Makkeh, Gutknecht and Wibral, defining paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.103.032149) · [Earlier project bound and counterexample](https://github.com/sepahead/pid-rs/blob/927e5138d23e0bd24b70f8df0fd38e610456a244/audit/archive/shared-exclusions-research-findings-20261001/source-redundancy.md.txt#L69-L102)

**When a simpler check suffices.** The equality $G=0$ holds exactly when $B=0$. To decide this, group the observations by $(S_1,S_2,T)$ and see whether each group has a single third-source label. Neither the entropy nor the 18 individual atoms need to be computed. A direct conditional entropy also gives the amount of remaining uncertainty about $S_3$. For a prediction decision one can compare the actual prediction errors with and without that source.

**Where the sharp bound helps.** If $B>0$, $N\ge2$, and there are $2\le\ell\le N$ occupied third labels, set $s=N-\ell+2$. The gap is at least
$$
\frac{2}{N}\ln\left[\left(1+\frac1{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}\right)
                       \left(1+\frac1{\lceil s/2\rceil}\right)\right].
$$
Tables attain this minimum when the other finite alphabets can be chosen for the construction. A program can therefore detect an impossible collection of signed atoms without knowing the right answer to each atom separately. For fixed $\ell$ the bound behaves as $8/N^2$ for large $N$. Repeating each observation preserves its empirical distribution and actual gap but changes the minimum allowed over all tables of the new size. This is a check on an empirical calculation. Sampling uncertainty requires separate evidence.

**A case where every ordinary entropy agrees.** Set $S_1=X$, $S_2$ to a constant, $S_3=W$ and $T=X$. Rows index $X$ and columns index $W$. Each positive cell in each matrix contributes one of four equiprobable observations.
$$
C_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
C_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Both row margins and both column margins are $(1,2,1)$. Thus $H(X)=H(W)=\tfrac32\ln 2$ and $H(X,W)=2\ln 2$. The constant source and the copied target determine every other subset entropy from these three values. All 16 agree, including the empty-subset entropy zero. In both tables $A=B=\tfrac12\ln2$, and the conditional mutual information from $W$ about $T$ given $X$ is zero. An observer with $X$ predicts $T$ perfectly in either table.

For an occupied cell, the event “$X$ matches or $W$ matches” has probability equal to its row count plus its column count minus one, divided by four. The four numerators are $1,2,2,3$ for the first table and $2,2,2,2$ for the second. This event is certain given the observed $T=X$. Its signed average is $R_1=\tfrac14\ln(64/3)$ or $R_2=\ln2$. The mutual information $I(X;W)$ is $\ln2$ in both tables. The only possible contributions to $M$ are $I(X;W)-R$ and its negative. Consequently $M_1=\tfrac12\ln(4/3)$ and $M_2=0$. The gaps are $G_1=\tfrac12\ln3$ and $G_2=\ln2$. The first attains the sharp positive lower gap at $N=4,\ell=3$. The second attains the largest gap allowed by $M\ge0$. These are opposite endpoints for the absolute mass at the same full vector of Shannon subset entropies. The raw incidence of the occupied row and column labels distinguishes the pair.

A different decomposition can give the same answer for both tables. Consider any ordinary two-source decomposition with four nonnegative mean parts. The part unique to $W$ plus the joint-only part equals $I(W;T\mid X)=0$. Both parts must therefore vanish. Its shared part is $I(W;T)=\ln2$, and the part unique to $X$ is $\tfrac12\ln2$. The signed shared-exclusions functional permits the negative and positive contributions that cancel in the first table. Chicharro, Pica and Panzeri discuss how assumptions about deterministic target–source relations affect the meaning of these terms. [Deterministic target–source identity, sections 3–4](https://doi.org/10.3390/e20030169)

**What the example adds.** James and Crutchfield already gave different distributions with equal Shannon information diagrams. They found several methods that separate their examples, including a partial information decomposition. The present example resolves a narrower question. It shows exactly how far this named five-atom Makkeh–Gutknecht–Wibral calculation can differ between two tables with the same Shannon vector at four observations and three occupied third labels. The example can detect software that silently replaces signed contributions by their conditional mutual information. These four observations could be synthetic sensor states. There is no improvement in prediction error to attribute to the decomposition on this task. [James and Crutchfield (2017), sections 2–3](https://doi.org/10.3390/e19100531)

For a copied target the qualitative sign pattern was already explicit in Makkeh, Gutknecht and Wibral’s RndErr. The two matching cells have mass 3/8 each and the two mismatches have mass 1/8 each. The negative unique mean of the noisy source is balanced by positive synergy. Finn and Lizier supplied the distribution and calculated their own distinct decomposition. The September project report already gives the two-source formula and factors the difference between numerator and denominator. Locating its two opposite terms among the five three-source positions gives the criterion for their individual zeros. The equal-entropy pair tests that distinction. ## A continuous angular population

Ehrlich and colleagues define a continuous shared-exclusions functional with a specified local length scale. The angular reference fixes one scale across three source angles and gives the full joint density of those angles and a target angle. The density has two cosine terms with amplitudes $a,b$ and is nonnegative when $r=|a|+|b|\le1$. Its probabilities, reference measure and angular units are known by construction. At a single input, direct numerical integration over the two cosine phases can avoid the full four-dimensional integral. A validated integral can give a certified answer at that input. Smooth periodic integrands also admit efficient trapezoidal rules under the applicable regularity assumptions. The allowed boundary includes zeros of the density and needs its own analysis. [Ehrlich et al., defining paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.110.014115) · [Full population density and integration at a zero](https://github.com/sepahead/pid-rs/blob/927e5138d23e0bd24b70f8df0fd38e610456a244/audit/research/angular-unit-boundary/EXPOSITION.md#L19-L50) · [Trefethen and Weideman, periodic quadrature](https://doi.org/10.1137/130932132)

The project result answers a different operational question. Suppose an evaluator approximates the joint term by a finite number of even moments and evaluates all proper terms exactly. How many moments guarantee relative error strictly below one part per million for the top atom at every $0<r\le1$ and in every direction? The least order is 7,278. At 7,277 the first axis fails at $r=1$. At four moments and $r=0.30$, the first-axis error exceeds 1.243953259800 parts per million and the second-axis error is below 0.751865782499. These opposite decisions give a short regression test. A few accurate quadrature evaluations do not prove the uniform statement. An independently certified quadrature can still be the better evaluator of an individual integral. The 7,278 is an order for this moment approximation. It places no lower bound on quadrature evaluations, sensor measurements or nearest-neighbour samples. A measured neural or robot data set still needs a justified population model, an identified scale and a sampling analysis. [Two adjacent orders and the two numerical axis cases](https://github.com/sepahead/pid-rs/blob/927e5138d23e0bd24b70f8df0fd38e610456a244/audit/research/angular-unit-boundary/EXPOSITION.md#L145-L157)

## Evidence

The paired-table calculation compares all 16 exact entropy products and all five mean signed positions with a separately implemented count of the original 18-node decomposition. It checks two local components at each of the 18 nodes in every occupied row. Ordinary and optimized interpreters returned the same exact rational factors and rejected the seven specified altered claims or inputs. The tables and predicted answers were known before this finite check. It is an exact check on deliberately constructed cases. A separate analytical note gives the condition on occupied cells for $M=0$ throughout the copy-target family. Each positive cell must be the sole occupied cell in its row or in its column. Separate full-node checks confirm this condition on 80 nonempty two-by-two count matrices, 728 two-by-three matrices and 511 binary three-by-three support patterns. Pools overlap after removal of unused labels. All of these comparisons share the same scientific definitions. No empirical superiority, priority search, kernel proof of these claims or human scientific review is established by this comparison.
