# What does one retained stimulus bit tell us about a model neuron?

Choose one of the 651 published control conditions with equal probability. The two inputs are nominal counts chosen by the investigator. One sets contacts on basal dendrites and the other on the apical tuft. The output is the number of simulated action potentials, grouped as zero, one to two, or three to four. These are the classes used by Kay, Schulz and Phillips (2022). The file gives one realized output per pair of settings. Trial-to-trial variation, population firing probabilities and intervention effects are not identified.

At acquisition time compute the single bit C which equals one if the nominal basal count B strictly exceeds the nominal tuft count A. A tie gives zero. This definition uses neither output nor random fitting. At analysis time keep two settings X=(floor(B/w),floor(A/w)) using nominal count widths w=10, 50 and 100. The 10-count step preserves every original setting. Keep the natural endpoints, including 300 or 200 in the final range even when it contains a single observed setting. Use w=100 as the main coarse comparison, w=50 as a fixed sensitivity analysis, and every result from all three widths. These choices and the equal grid prior define the problem. They are project choices, not properties of natural stimulation. Random selection of nominal synapses in the original simulator can repeat an index; the axis values specify requested input settings.

Questions. Can the one-bit metadata change the optimal three-class prediction or improve a fitted prediction when an entire input level is withheld? What can each named comparator establish about the use of the bit?

Only the source distribution and the same target-dependent finite distribution enter the categorical Makkeh–Gutknecht–Wibral (MGW) functional. No continuous estimator, nearest-neighbour search or fitted quantizer enters it. A reference outcome is one supported combination of the three retained source values and the output class. Count true source matches under each of the 18 rules. Calculate averages with the number of original settings represented by the outcome. The five positions outside the two-input downset are the rules {3}, {1,3}, {2,3}, {{1,3},{2,3}}, and {1,2,3}, where a single displayed source set is the one member of that rule. Give all five informative, misinformative and signed products, not just selected signs. Use natural logarithms and normalize by 651. The number D is the sum of the magnitudes of the negative signed mean atoms among the five. It equals half of the difference between their absolute sum and the ordinary conditional information. D identifies within-decomposition cancellation. It has no independent units of task utility.

Compare the five informative and misinformative sums to H(C|X) and H(C|X,T), and the signed sum to their difference. Compare all five signed positions against Williams–Beer minimum specific information (I_min) on exactly the same finite distribution, with the same node order and labels. Its nonnegative atoms and MGW signed atoms have different definitions. Compare a saturated empirical optimal classifier on X vs (X,C), with unit cost per wrong three-class decision and ties assigned the lowest class. Give integer correct counts and the error rate saved in each bin pair; a nonnegative per-acquisition cost lambda, in units of one wrong decision, gives the exact range in which reading C lowers expected loss. Indifference applies at equality. Also compare fitted conditional histograms with add-one smoothing on the three target categories, leaving out one entire original basal input level at a time and, separately, one entire tuft level at a time. Fit distinct predictors with and without the flag. Empty cells get probability 1/3. Each of the 651 settings is tested once under each blocking choice. Report all probabilities sufficient to verify aggregate log losses, correct counts, training/evaluation roles and denominators. These held-out settings repeat the same simulation grid. No independent biological replication, estimated timing in a device or best-in-class accuracy is claimed. Direct per-bin gains and a Bayes decision are proper comparators even if the PID adds no decision advantage.
