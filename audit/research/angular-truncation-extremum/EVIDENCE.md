## Why the earlier proof also works

Keep the report's definitions. The mixed joint contribution is at least k₁r²y². Compare H(rx)+k₁r²y² with B₂H(r) one order at a time. At j=1 the excess is k₁r²[h₁(B₁−B₂)+(1−h₁)y²]. The j=2 term can lose at most 4k₂h₂r⁴y² since B₂−x⁴=(4x²+y²)y²≤4y². Every j≥3 term has coefficient k_jh_j times $x^{2j}-B_2$, with both factors after k_j nonpositive. Thus the denominator difference is at least r²y²[7/72−(7/1728)r²] and hence at least (161/1728)b² for 0<r<1. This suffices for strict directional dominance. The termwise public proof additionally retains the nonnegative joint coefficients from j≥2.

The alternative factor B₁ fails at a=99999/1000000 and b=1/1000000. A separate closed-form check gives a strictly negative upper bound for A−B₁H. This counterexample applies to the intermediate denominator inequality only. It does not claim failure of the directional theorem. [Finite input, exact output and scopes](certificate.json)

## Twelve routes considered

| Route | Required premise and evidence | Decision and failure condition |
|---|---|---|
| Retain the 23 November range | The previous lower/upper certificates reproduce. | Preserves the older bound. It leaves the exact worst direction undecided. |
| Direct conditional mutual information | A question asks about increment from sources 2 and 3 after source 1. | On the failing axis the answer is zero. This quantity cannot replace the chosen atom in an approximation test. |
| Direct held-out control or prediction loss | A physical task, outcomes and a test design exist. | Use for a sensor decision. No such empirical inputs occur in this study. |
| Uniform grid of directions | A false continuous assertion can have a sampled counterexample. | Useful for plotting. A finite grid alone leaves directions between points unchecked. |
| Quadrature in two phases | A rigorously controlled integration error is available. | A reference check is possible. Plain numerical integration cannot certify the continuum of radii. |
| Finer grid with the older uniform bound | The bound is valid and the grid resolves it. | The analytic slack persists as grid spacing tends to zero. |
| Axial root by itself | A closed-form numerator and denominator determine the one-axis threshold. | It is an upper bound for all-direction acceptance until a directional proof is supplied. |
| Maximize absolute error in each joint moment | Squared binomial masses are at most one. | Both pure axes maximize the numerator. The two denominators differ. |
| Use the first binomial factor for both sides | The stronger denominator comparison must hold. | Strict rational counterexample in the report. An additional restricted parameter domain would need its own proof. |
| Use positivity of all axial coefficients | All h_j must be nonnegative. | Direct evaluation gives h_j<0 from j=3. |
| Use a derivative of the two-dimensional quotient | One needs a controlled derivative for every mixture and both axes. | Unnecessary here after the common second factor and strict scalar ordering. |
| Use one shared second factor and compare radial series | All signs and both homogeneous denominator terms are explicit. | Selected. A changed gauge, functional, truncated proper term or joint law requires a new derivation. |
