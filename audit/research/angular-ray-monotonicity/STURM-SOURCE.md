# An exact root count for the two fixed four-moment decisions

This companion checks two specified amplitudes in the fixed radian-coordinate angular model. The two phase cosines are independent under the uniform reference measure. The underlying joint density, exact terms for all proper source groups, and four-term approximation to the full joint term are those of the angular study. The result concerns a population integral. Let the two amplitudes be a and b. Their absolute sum is r. For r>0 define the direction x=|a|/r. It lies between 0 and 1. Write A for the exact positive highest-order atom and T for the positive omitted part after four even terms. The tolerance ε is one part per million, or 1/1,000,000. The sign of T−εA determines whether this approximation is above or below that tolerance. Every sign choice of a and b is covered by the symmetry of the stated phase model.

The checker expands the source moment at even order 2j as a polynomial in x. Put k(j)=binom(2j,j)/[4^j·2j(2j−1)]. Let B(j,x) be the sum, for i=0 through j, of the squares of binom(j,i)x^i(1−x)^(j−i). Let h(j)=2(2j+1)/4^j−(j+1)(4/9)^j. The order-2j contribution to the exact atom is k(j)r^(2j)[B(j,x)+(h(j)−1)x^(2j)]. Only the B term at orders j>4 contributes to T. These formulas follow from the even cosine moments and the exact proper terms of this fixed model.

Take the prefix through j=18. At every omitted order the normalized coefficient of T−εA is (1−ε)B(j,x)+ε[1−h(j)]x^(2j). It is nonnegative because h(j)<0 for j≥19. The binomial probabilities sum to one, so 0≤B(j,x)≤1. The proper weight satisfies |h(j)|≤2. The k(j) are positive and decreasing. Thus the omitted part lies between zero and (1+2ε)k(19)r^38/(1−r²). This bound holds at the two stated radii, both strictly below one.

At r=340315168725/10^12 and x=21787956/10^8, exact rational evaluation and this tail bound give −86262≤10^24(T−εA)≤−86092. Thus this one direction has relative error strictly below the tolerance.

At r=340315168726/10^12 the 18-term prefix is a polynomial of degree 36. Its constant positive scale can be cleared to give integer coefficients. Both endpoint values are strictly positive. To test the interior, start the **Sturm sequence** with this polynomial and its derivative. Each later term is the negative remainder from dividing the previous two. Multiplication by positive integers and division by positive greatest common divisors keep all signs unchanged. Count the changes of sign at each endpoint after omitting zero entries. Sturm's theorem makes their difference the number of distinct interior roots. Here the sequence ends in a nonzero constant, and both counts equal 18. The polynomial has no root on the interval, so it is positive throughout. Its nonnegative tail preserves strict positivity for every x, including the two axes. This certifies both finite decisions.
