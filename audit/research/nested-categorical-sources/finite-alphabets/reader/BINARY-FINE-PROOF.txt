# Sharp least positive directed informative gap with at most two fine labels

Independent proposed result before looking at another proposed restricted-alphabet result or the earlier enumerated binary numeric outputs. Let N be an integer count total, X the proposed coarse variable, Y the designated fine variable with at most two occupied labels and D=C_X^+ - C_{{X},{Y}}^+ for the finite empirical Makkeh–Gutknecht–Wibral shared-exclusions construction. A failed map Y→X means D>0 and N≥2. The target and all other sources are unrestricted and do not enter D. The sharp values are
[
   G=\exp(ND)\ge \frac{2N}{N-1},\qquad
   D\ge \frac1N\ln\frac{2N}{N-1}.
]
For every N≥2 choose two coarse labels, one shared fine label on which each coarse label occurs once, and a second fine label on which the remaining N-2 observations all have the same one of these coarse labels. Omit the empty column when N=2. The product is 2N/(N-1). This is the only form of equality up to relabeling of occupied categories.

Write a_i, b_j, c_ij for row, column and cell counts. Each observation in cell (i,j) contributes factor 1+(b_j-c_ij)/a_i to G. Say a fine column is split if it meets at least two occupied coarse rows. If both columns are split, every observed cell has another row at the same fine value, so the product in any coarse row of size a is at least (1+1/a)^a≥2 by the binomial formula. There are at least two coarse rows, hence G≥4. Two split columns require N≥4, when 4>2N/(N-1). This argument allows arbitrarily many coarse labels.

In the remaining case there is exactly one split fine column, and at most one other column, pure. Choose a coarse row distinct from that of the pure column; if there is no pure column, choose any coarse row in the split column. This row has size b≥1 and all of its observations occur in the split column. At least one other coarse row appears in that column. Its b factors have product at least (1+1/b)^b≥2. Choose one observation of another row in the split column. Let its whole row have size a≤N-b. That observation adds a distinct factor at least 1+b/a≥1+b/(N-b)=N/(N-b)≥N/(N-1). All other factors are at least one. This proves the bound. Equality forces b=1, a=N-1 and exactly one other observation in the split column. If any more were present, the factor from the sole observation in the small row would exceed 2 (or another would give a strict factor above 1). Thus that entire column has one count from each of precisely two coarse rows; all other counts are in the one pure column, as claimed.

There is no failed map at N=0 (no distribution) or N=1. For exactly two occupied fine labels, failure requires N≥3 and equality above is possible at every such N. If only one fine label is allowed, D=H(X), and G=N^N/∏_i a_i^{a_i}. Merging coarse rows increases this denominator; with at least two rows, redistributing counts from a smaller row to a larger row also increases the denominator because t log t is strictly convex. The least product in the one-column class is therefore N^N/(N-1)^{N-1} with coarse margins N-1 and 1. At N=2 or N=3 the universal unrestricted-fine-alphabet result agrees with the at-most-binary result. At N≥4 its equality criterion needs three occupied fine labels, so it is strictly lower. The restricted gap in nats is asymptotic to (ln 2)/N, while the unrestricted gap is asymptotic to 4/N². Neither has a positive lower bound independent of N. These statements are about exact observed label counts and the named informative-component difference. No inference about unknown population support or about signed atoms or another PID follows.
