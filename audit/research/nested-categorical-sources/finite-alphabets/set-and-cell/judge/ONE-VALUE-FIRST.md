# One occupied fine value

If every row has the same fine reading, its match event is the entire support. The difference D is the Shannon entropy of the coarse reading. With N>=2 unit observations and a violated coarse map, the least positive D is [N log N-(N-1)log(N-1)]/N. Equality requires coarse counts 1 and N-1. Indeed, merging any two positive coarse groups decreases their entropy strictly. Among two groups with proportions x and 1-x, with 0<x<=1/2, the derivative of binary entropy is log((1-x)/x), positive on (0,1/2). The minimum therefore has x=1/N. The corresponding product is N^N/(N-1)^(N-1). This stratum overlaps the two-available-label equality case at N=2.

The lower thresholds concern actual tables with unit observation weights. With two occupied coarse and exactly two fine values, put cell probabilities 1/2-epsilon, epsilon, epsilon, 1/2-epsilon around a two-by-two table, 0<epsilon<1/2. Both margins are 1/2. At the two majority cells the gained mass is epsilon. At the other cells the gained mass is 1/2-epsilon. Hence D=(1-2epsilon)log(1+2epsilon)+2epsilon log(2-2epsilon) tends to zero. A split of positive mass alone supplies no universal positive population margin even with fixed balanced binary marginals.
