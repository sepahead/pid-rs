# A sharp observed difference when the fine reading has at most two values

Question opened after a reader observed that the unrestricted sharp bound can require a third fine category. The outcome of the earlier general bound, the four-cell real-mass example and the earlier enumeration through six rows have been seen. This is a new exploratory mathematical derivation. No blind selection or population inference is claimed. It concerns only the informative cumulative for the finite categorical shared-exclusions definition in the indicated direction. The target drops out of this component.

Let N be the integer number of observations. Each has one coarse value and one of at most two occupied fine values. The coarse alphabet can be any finite set. A split fine value meets two or more coarse values. Write E=exp(ND). At a cell in a coarse group of size a, one observation contributes the factor 1+h/a, where h counts observations of its fine value in all other coarse groups. The exact product over observations is E. With a split, N is at least two and there are at least two occupied coarse groups. I obtain E >= 2N/(N-1). When N=2 the only failure has both unit observations at one fine value and different coarse values. For N>2, equality has one coarse group of size one and a second of size N-1. The fine value joining the groups contains exactly one observation from each. All remaining observations are in the second group at a private second fine value. Coarse and fine value labels can be permuted.

Proof. If each occupied fine value is split, every observation gains at least one. For a coarse fibre of size a the product is at least (1+1/a)^a >= 2 by the binomial theorem. At least two coarse fibres give E >=4. This exceeds 2N/(N-1) for N>2. With two rows equality gives exactly the already stated table. Otherwise exactly one of the at most two fine values is split and exactly one other fine value is private to a coarse group A. Each other occupied coarse group B lies wholly inside the split fine value. Let b=|B| and let h be the number of observations of that shared value outside B. Its b factors give (1+h/b)^b >= 1+h >=2. Select one split observation in a different coarse group. Its group has size at most N-1, and it gains at least one observation, so its factor is at least N/(N-1). All other factors are at least one. Equality requires h=1, b=1 by strictness of the binomial expansion for b>1. Equality in the other factor also requires that its coarse group have size N-1. The shared fine fibre then has size two; both other groups and other shared observations are excluded. Conversely the proposed table attains E. The case of one occupied fine value belongs to the all-split case when the map fails. With one row no violation exists. The proof covers fixed binary fine alphabets as well as a single occupied fine value in a possibly larger declared alphabet, but attainment for N>2 requires that two distinct fine values be allowed. The precise one-occupied-fine stratum needs separate treatment for a sharp threshold.

A direct map check finds violations without arithmetic. In the two-bin setting the infimum of the positive gap scales as ln(2)/N, whereas the unrestricted finite-alphabet sharp lower bound is asymptotically 4/N squared. These asymptotic rates follow from continuity of the logarithm and its derivative at one and have separate premises. Recheck the strict-inequality thresholds, equality class and scope by independent derivation and integer tables before publication.
