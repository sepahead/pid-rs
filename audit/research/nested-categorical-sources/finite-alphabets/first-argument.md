# First derivation, before comparison with another fixed-binary result

For every integer total N≥2, among failed maps Y→X with at most two occupied fine labels Y and at least two permitted coarse labels X, I get the minimum G=2N/(N−1). It is attained by two coarse rows with counts (1,N−2) and (1,0), dropping the second column if N=2. The minimum information deficit is ln[2N/(N−1)]/N nats. At N=1 or with one available coarse class a failure is impossible. Exactly two occupied Y permit failure only from N=3; there the same witness applies. In particular N=4 gives 8/3 rather than 9/4. Its leading asymptotic deficit is ln(2)/N.

Let B_y denote any fixed column total. For an occupied cell in row i with margin r_i and count c_iy, the factor is 1+(B_y−c_iy)/r_i, raised to c_iy. Merge two distinct coarse rows. Each old observation in those rows now has a denominator at least as large and a nonnegative numerator at most as large, so no factor rises; all other rows keep their factors. On a failed table choose two rows A,H that both meet a column. If there are more than two rows, merge every surplus row into A, leaving H distinct. The chosen cell in A still has an observation of H in its column. Its positive foreign count, divided by a strictly larger row margin, strictly reduces its factor, even if the numerator stays fixed. The map remains failed. Thus no table with more than two occupied X values is a minimizer.

With two rows, if both fine columns contain both rows, write their row margins as r,s≥2. Every observation has at least one foreign observation in its column. Hence G≥(1+1/r)^r(1+1/s)^s≥(1+1)^2=4 by the binomial lower bound (indeed both analogues are strictly greater than 2). This requires N≥4 and gives a strict value above 2N/(N−1)≤8/3. If exactly one column is ambiguous, exchange row names so that all counts in the other column (possibly empty) belong to the first row. Let a,c≥1 be the first and second row counts in the ambiguous column, and N−a−c≥0 the remaining pure count. The product is
G=(1+c/(N−c))^a(1+a/c)^c ≥ [N/(N−c)]·(1+1/c)^c ≥ [N/(N−1)]·2.
The first inequality is strict unless a=1; the second uses c≥1 and (1+1/c)^c≥1+c/c=2. If c≥2, the binomial quadratic term is positive and N/(N−c)>N/(N−1). Thus equality requires a=c=1, and the displayed triangular witness attains it. At N=2 it has only the one shared column; at totals above two it has exactly two occupied columns. No binomial monotonicity lemma is needed.

If only one fine value is permitted at a larger fixed N, a different sharp result is N^N/(N−1)^(N−1). Here G=N^N/Π_i r_i^{r_i}. The same strict coarse merge leaves two positive rows. For r+s=N, the convex function x ln x on positive values maximizes r ln r+s ln s at an endpoint r=1 or s=1. This case exceeds the two-label optimum for N>2. The unrestricted sharp margin is achievable with one or two columns at N=2,3 but requires a third private label from N=4. Steps here use real/integer arithmetic and an exact count table, without a population sampling rule or kernel result. Independent brute census is still pending.
