# Evidence for three nested categorical sources

**Sepehr Mahmoudian · 25 November 2026.** The [standalone scientific report](EXPOSITION.md) gives the definitions, general proofs, finite counterexamples and resource limits. The research question concerns three deterministic source resolutions and any finite target. It asks how many of the 108 named categorical products can change and how small a failed source-map test can be.

A stochastic channel, observed entropy order, target-copy axiom, independent target or particular source names do not supply these maps. A fixed coarse-to-fine order permutes the original named source masks. Reads at equal resolution can be dependent or identical. ## Alternatives, assumptions and failure checks

We compared thirteen routes before the first 60-case calculation. Retaining the existing open decision was appropriate for unsupported general claims. Conditional entropies and conditional mutual information were the simplest numerical comparators. They give the levels but do not place all 18 named nodes. The general 108-coordinate calculation was retained as a judge. Proving the general parser, giving new general logarithm enclosures, or enrolling observed data would have required different source or sampling premises. Six existing case files alone would not exercise all strict levels. Copies alone would miss genuine refinements. An entropy order and a stochastic channel can both hold without rowwise nesting. Signed sparsity can occur with crossed inputs. A new coupling would be needed for a near-nesting claim. We selected deterministic source maps, named products, a direct column scan, and set-based event arithmetic with adverse inputs. Reconsider the alternatives when the target concern, support, data, general certificate or acquisition assumptions change.
