# Finite integer bounds for the logarithm series

A logarithm converts a product of positive rational factors into a sum of information values. The original six-table reader uses a finite series to enclose this logarithm. This package proves that its model of the integer loop encloses every exact finite partial sum. The numerator and denominator of the partial sum are defined by an independent recurrence. The accompanying article gives the real logarithm and the final signed interval their own mathematical proofs. [Study](../EXPOSITION.md)

## Formal scope

The source [Model.lean](Model.lean) defines the scaled integer powers, the two sums and the exact rational recurrence. [Proof.lean](Proof.lean) contains thirteen theorems. Each denominator used to interpret a fraction is positive. The full-prefix theorem works for every nonnegative numerator, positive denominator, bit count and iteration count. The production range constrains the numerator to at most one third of the denominator for the subsequent real remainder. The zero input returns the exact pair of zeros. The positive input adds one integer unit to the final upper sum. The full rounded interval can be wider than one unit.

[Observer.lean](Observer.lean) applies the statements to 22 typed and concrete examples, including a positive input outside the real-tail range. It prints the thirteen full declaration statements, terms and transitive axiom records. The two terms for addition of intervals and for the lower product use propositional extensionality, which identifies logically equivalent propositions. Each of the eleven other terms also uses quotient soundness, which identifies quotient images of related representatives. The recorded terms do not use a choice axiom.
