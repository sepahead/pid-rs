# Time and case outcomes

The proof judge uses one sequential worker. It must check 32 complete scientific modules in each of three cases. One complete module needs a compile and a separate stock fresh replay. The cases also need 27, 19 and 26 isolated proof elaborations and three diagnostic compiles each.

The earlier original run spent 188.00 seconds before its first W01 compile. The archive-transfer progress messages span 4.46 seconds. The separate stock extraction took 7.39 seconds. The 21 artificial-control commands totaled 165.09 seconds. These times overlap the 188.00-second startup interval. Nineteen successful W01 fresh checks ranged from 44.47 to 51.37 seconds. Their median was 45.02 seconds. Their matching 19 compiles totaled 18.46 seconds. The next compile ran for 428.17 seconds and reached the memory limit. It gave no completion time for that proof. The original W03 and W15 jobs never started. [Original command and job receipts](https://github.com/sepahead/pid-rs/actions/runs/44760935574)

In a later successful artificial run, nine stock fresh checks took 55.38 to 57.50 seconds. Their median was 56.20 seconds. A separate 44,089-byte artificial script had a final consumer fresh check of 58.46 seconds. Its definitions and finite observation differ from the three original scripts. The later artificial trace took 0.26 seconds to print. There is no matching original timing for any of the 72 isolated proofs, the three full finite traces or the later original proof terms. The historical runs did not supply a comparable proof of single-CPU affinity. Suppose the 96 original fresh checks each take 56.20 seconds, the old W01 startup time applies once and each case takes the old 18.46 seconds for its 19 initial compiles. Using the unrounded measured times gives 5,638.34 seconds before the other 39 compiles, 72 isolated elaborations and nine diagnostics. With the old W01 fresh median, the corresponding subtotal is 4,564.98 seconds. A 5,400-second aggregate would leave only 835.02 seconds for these 120 operations in the second scenario. Neither calculation predicts the runtime of an untried proof.

The new aggregate limit is 18,000 seconds. It comprises 1,500 seconds for shared setup, up to 5,400 seconds for each of W01, W03 and W15, and 300 seconds for final custody. The job timeout is 320 minutes. A registered case failure can release unused time but cannot increase the next case's 5,400-second window. The process and memory limits remain 900 seconds and 4 GiB. All cases share the 1 GiB retained-evidence limit. The hosted runner limit permits this one job. [GitHub Actions limits](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/limits)

At the 56.20-second fresh median and the earlier prefix compile total, a 5,400-second case window leaves approximately 83.33, 102.38 and 85.31 seconds per remaining unmeasured command in W01, W03 and W15, respectively. Real times can differ substantially. An original proof can still exceed its own clock or memory limit. The case record keeps that failure. The next case requires a clean process, fresh case directory, checked common supplier and intact inputs. All three must independently pass before the judge accepts the three-case result.
