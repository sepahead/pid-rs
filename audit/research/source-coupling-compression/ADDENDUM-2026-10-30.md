# Addendum to the source-partition report

Sepehr Mahmoudian · 30 October 2026

Status: dated addendum to [the report of 24 October 2026](EXPOSITION.md). It repairs two replay
scripts, adds one exact lower bound and one tighter upper bound, and corrects how some empirical
results should be read. The report's Markdown and PDF are unchanged; they take these changes at
their next republication, which needs a new visual review. The derivations below are written by
hand and checked by exact or binary64 computation. No formal proof, population, calibration or
priority claim is made.

## Terms and notation

The report and this addendum use the following terms. Standard names are given where they exist.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Distribution | A joint probability distribution of finitely many variables, each with finitely many values. The report calls a distribution of the study data a *law*; its 30 laws are the 30 empirical distributions listed in its section 6. |
| Sources $S=(S_1,S_2,S_3)$, target $T$ | The study's three binary input variables and the occupancy variable. $S_1$ is the light bit; $(S_2,S_3)$ is the pair that the code compresses. |
| Recoded sources $S'$ | $S'=(S_1,g(S_2,S_3))$: the code keeps the two pairs most frequent in training (here $00$ and $11$) and maps every other pair to $00$. $S$, $S'$ and $T$ are observed together on the same rows. |
| Redundancy lattice | The 18 antichains (nonempty families of nonempty source sets, none containing another) of Williams and Beer, ordered as in the report. The report calls it the *carrier*. |
| Redundancy value, atom | At each lattice node, MGW shared-exclusions redundancy is a number in nats; the report calls it a *cumulative*. Möbius inversion turns the 18 redundancy values into 18 *atoms*, so each redundancy value is the sum of the atoms at and below its node. |
| Informative and misinformative parts | MGW write each redundancy value as an informative part minus a misinformative part. Both parts have their own atoms, and MGW Theorem IV.3 proves that these component atoms are never negative. The signed (net) atom is their difference and can be negative. |
| $\Delta_\alpha$, $D$ | $\Delta_\alpha$ is the old atom minus the new atom at node $\alpha$; $D=\sum_\alpha\lvert\Delta_\alpha\rvert$ is the *allocation movement*, in nats. |
| $\ell_B$ | For a nonempty source set $B\subseteq\{1,2,3\}$, $\ell_B=I(S_B;T)-I(S'_B;T)$, the change of the mutual information carried by that set; $\ell_\varnothing=0$. |
| Tail mass $\delta$ | The fraction of rows whose pair $(S_2,S_3)$ is not kept by the code. |

All logarithms are natural, so every information value is in nats.

## 2. The best lower bound from the seven information changes

The report's lower bound (8) splits the 18 atoms into two groups. All seven changes $\ell_B$
together give a larger bound, and its exact value has a closed form.

Write $J_B$ for the set of nodes at or below the one-collection node $\{B\}$. The report shows that
$\sum_{\alpha\in J_B}\Delta_\alpha=\ell_B$ for every $B$. Collect these seven equations as
$A\Delta=\ell$, where $A$ is the $7\times18$ matrix with $A_{B\alpha}=1$ when $\alpha\in J_B$ and
$0$ otherwise. A *maximal chain* is a sequence $\{i\}\subset\{i,j\}\subset\{1,2,3\}$ with
$i\ne j$; there are six.

**Proposition 1.** For every $\ell\in\mathbb R^7$,
$$
\min_{d\in\mathbb R^{18}:\,Ad=\ell}\ \sum_\alpha\lvert d_\alpha\rvert
=\max_{i\ne j}\Bigl(\lvert\ell_{\{i\}}\rvert+\lvert\ell_{\{i,j\}}-\ell_{\{i\}}\rvert
+\lvert\ell_{\{1,2,3\}}-\ell_{\{i,j\}}\rvert\Bigr).
$$
Since the true change vector $\Delta$ satisfies $A\Delta=\ell$, the right side is a lower bound on
$D$.

*Proof of "at least".* Along a maximal chain the node sets are nested:
$J_{\{i\}}\subseteq J_{\{i,j\}}\subseteq J_{\{1,2,3\}}$, and the last one is the whole lattice. So
$\ell_{\{i\}}$, $\ell_{\{i,j\}}-\ell_{\{i\}}$ and $\ell_{\{1,2,3\}}-\ell_{\{i,j\}}$ are sums of $d$
over three disjoint node sets. The triangle inequality bounds each absolute value by the sum of
$\lvert d_\alpha\rvert$ over its set, and the three sets together are part of the lattice.

*Proof of "at most".* Define a number for every source set, by increasing size: $v(\varnothing)=0$
and $v(B)=\max_{C\subsetneq B}\bigl(v(C)+\max\{\ell_C-\ell_B,0\}\bigr)$. Put $u(B)=\ell_B+v(B)$.

1. *Both are nondecreasing.* For $C\subsetneq B$, the definition gives $v(B)\ge v(C)$. It also gives
   $v(B)\ge v(C)+\ell_C-\ell_B$, so $u(B)=\ell_B+v(B)\ge\ell_C+v(C)=u(C)$. Both vanish at
   $\varnothing$, so both are nonnegative.
2. *A nondecreasing set function is a sum of nonnegative atoms.* Let $f\ge0$ be nondecreasing with
   $f(\varnothing)=0$. For a level $s\ge0$, the sets $B$ with $f(B)>s$ form a family that is closed
   upwards; its minimal members form an antichain $\alpha_s$, which is a lattice node when the family
   is not empty. A set $B$ belongs to the family exactly when some member of $\alpha_s$ is contained
   in $B$, that is, exactly when $\alpha_s\in J_B$. Give each node $\alpha$ the weight
   $p_\alpha\ge0$ equal to the length of the set of levels $s$ with $\alpha_s=\alpha$. Then
   $\sum_{\alpha\in J_B}p_\alpha$ is the length of the levels $s$ below $f(B)$, which is $f(B)$, and
   $\sum_\alpha p_\alpha=f(\{1,2,3\})$.
3. *A feasible vector of the right size.* Apply step 2 to $u$ and to $v$ and put $d=p(u)-p(v)$.
   Then $Ad=u-v=\ell$, and

   $$
   \sum_\alpha\lvert d_\alpha\rvert\le u(\{1,2,3\})+v(\{1,2,3\})
   =\ell_{\{1,2,3\}}+2v(\{1,2,3\}).
   $$

4. *The value.* Unrolling the definition, $v(\{1,2,3\})$ is the largest total decrease of $\ell$
   along a chain of strictly increasing sets from $\varnothing$ to $\{1,2,3\}$. Inserting an
   intermediate set never lowers a total decrease, because
   $\max\{x-z,0\}\le\max\{x-y,0\}+\max\{y-z,0\}$, so the largest value occurs on a maximal chain. On any chain the total
   variation equals the net change $\ell_{\{1,2,3\}}$ plus twice the total decrease. So the bound
   of step 3 equals the largest total variation over the six maximal chains. $\square$

The same proof works for any number of sources, with the maximal chains of all source sets.

**Exact certificates.** For a chain $(i,j)$ and signs $s_1,s_2,s_3\in\{-1,1\}$, put
$y=(s_1-s_2)\,e_{\{i\}}+(s_2-s_3)\,e_{\{i,j\}}+s_3\,e_{\{1,2,3\}}$. For a node in $J_{\{i\}}$,
$(A^\top y)_\alpha=s_1$; in $J_{\{i,j\}}$ but not $J_{\{i\}}$ it is $s_2$; otherwise it is $s_3$.
So $\lvert(A^\top y)_\alpha\rvert\le1$ everywhere, and for every vector $d$ with $Ad=\ell$,
$y^\top\ell=(A^\top y)^\top d\le\sum_\alpha\lvert d_\alpha\rvert$; with $d=\Delta$ this gives
$y^\top\ell\le D$. [`verification/chain_lower_certificate.py`](verification/chain_lower_certificate.py)
checks all 48 such vectors exactly on the 18 nodes, evaluates each one at the saved outward interval
endpoints of the seven information changes, and keeps the largest result. The bounds are rational
numbers; no floating point enters them. They exceed the report's saved lower bound on six of the 30
distributions and equal it on the others:

| Distribution | Report bound (8) | Chain certificate | $D$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Office, 4 February 2015 | 0.079376 | 0.079596 | 0.765299 |
| Office, 5 February 2015 | 0.381779 | 0.394514 | 0.927480 |
| Office, 6 February 2015 | 0.068741 | 0.119510 | 0.568537 |
| Office, 9 February 2015 | 0.540707 | 0.541428 | 1.549248 |
| Office, 10 February 2015 | 0.097649 | 0.150664 | 0.514408 |
| Office validation | 0.402366 | 0.434641 | 1.336128 |

A lower bound can reject a claim that the recoding preserves the atoms, when it exceeds a declared
tolerance. It cannot confirm such a claim.

## 3. A tighter upper bound from nonnegative component atoms

For a one-collection node $\{B\}$ the informative redundancy value is $H(S_B)$ and the
misinformative one is $H(S_B\mid T)$. By MGW Theorem IV.3 the informative atoms $\pi^+$ and the
misinformative atoms $\pi^-$ are nonnegative. So each of the four component atom vectors of the old
and the new distribution lies in a polytope of the form $\{\pi\in\mathbb R^{18}:\pi\ge0,\ A\pi=h\}$,
with $h_B$ equal to $H(S_B)$, $H(S_B\mid T)$, $H(S'_B)$ or $H(S'_B\mid T)$. The change vector is
$\Delta=(\pi^{+}-\pi^{-})-(\pi'^{+}-\pi'^{-})$. Therefore $D$ lies between the smallest and the
largest value of $\sum_\alpha\lvert\Delta_\alpha\rvert$ over the product of the four polytopes.
Only the 28 coalition entropies are needed. The top row of $A$ is all ones, so every
$\pi^+$ sums to $H(S)$, and so on; the largest value is therefore never above the report's range
bound (11), $H(S)+H(S\mid T)+H(S')+H(S'\mid T)$.

The smallest value equalled the chain certificate of section 2 on all 30 distributions. The
largest value was computed as a mixed-integer linear program with the HiGHS solver in binary64
arithmetic. That is an exploratory computation, not a certificate. It was below the range bound on
all 30 distributions:

| Pooled distribution | $D$ | Range bound (11) | Largest value over the polytopes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Room, training | 0.309875 | 3.408395 | 2.267403 |
| Room, validation | 0 | 1.911905 | 0 |
| Room, test | 0.341754 | 2.311007 | 1.613103 |
| Office, training | 0.421590 | 5.059041 | 5.035341 |
| Office, validation | 1.336128 | 3.359736 | 3.281032 |
| Office, test | 0.582280 | 4.354175 | 3.747458 |

On the 21 distributions with movement it is lower than the range bound by 0.5% (office training) to
40% (office, 10 February 2015). The gain is real but modest, and the bound stays far above $D$: these entropy constraints say
nothing about which events the atoms share. The program is kept as
[`verification/component-sandwich-exploratory.py.txt`](verification/component-sandwich-exploratory.py.txt);
it needs NumPy and SciPy and is not a repository command.

## 4. A form of the coupling bound that uses the target

The refinement step in Proof 4 of the report gives slightly more than its bound (6). Write
$a_{\beta,T}=\sum_{B\in\beta}H(S'_B\mid S_B,T)$ and $b_{\beta,T}=\sum_{B\in\beta}H(S_B\mid S'_B,T)$.
Let $Z=((S_1,S'_1),(S_2,S'_2),(S_3,S'_3))$ be the common refinement of the report's Proof 4. From $S$
to $Z$ the informative redundancy value at $\beta$ rises by $u\in[0,a_\beta]$ and the misinformative one
by $u_T\in[0,a_{\beta,T}]$; from $S'$ to $Z$ they rise by $v\in[0,b_\beta]$ and $v_T\in[0,b_{\beta,T}]$.
The redundancy value is the informative part minus the misinformative part, so its change from
$S$ to $S'$ is $(v-v_T)-(u-u_T)=v-u+u_T-v_T$. Hence it lies in
$[-a_\beta-b_{\beta,T},\ b_\beta+a_{\beta,T}]$, and
$$
D\le\sum_\beta c_\beta\max\{a_\beta+b_{\beta,T},\ b_\beta+a_{\beta,T}\}.
$$
Conditioning cannot increase entropy, so this is never weaker than bound (1) of the report, and no
deterministic recoding is assumed. The review of 28 October proposed it. It improves bound (1)
strictly on 13 of the 30 distributions, by small amounts (room test: 16.650 to 16.582 nats), and it
stays above the range bound on every distribution with $D>0$.

## 5. A third upper bound in the saved records

The saved record of each distribution also contains `continuity_upper_interval`. `run_study_v3.py` computes it as
$$
130\,\min\{\ln8,\ \delta\ln(8/\delta)+8\delta\},
$$
where $\delta$ is the tail mass, 8 is the number of possible source tuples and 130 is twice the
total absolute Möbius coefficient 65 of the lattice. It comes from an earlier local note on rare-tuple
compression and is not discussed in the report. The run only checks that it lies above the
measured movement. On the 21 distributions with $D>0$ it lies between 49.3 and 270.3 nats, far above
the other upper bounds, so it says nothing useful about these distributions.

## 6. How to read the empirical results

**The evaluation days differ from the training days.** The code and the thresholds were fitted on
the training days. On later days the data moved:

| Recording, role | Rows | Tail mass $\delta$ | Constant sources |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Room, training | 7,021 | 0.083 | none |
| Room, validation | 1,063 | 0 | $S_2$, $S_3$ |
| Room, test | 2,045 | 0.045 | none |
| Office, training | 8,794 | 0.161 | none |
| Office, validation | 2,014 | 0.545 | none |
| Office, test | 9,752 | 0.490 | $S_2$ |

On every office test row the carbon-dioxide bit is 1: the training median (455 ppm) lies below
all test values. Half of the office validation and test rows therefore fall outside the two pairs
that the code keeps. The office movements are measurements of a training-frozen code under this
shift, not of compression in general.

**Why no information is lost on the office validation and test days.** On the test days only the
pairs $10$ and $11$ occur, and the code maps them to $00$ and $11$, so it is one-to-one there. On the
validation days the code merges $10$ with $00$, but only rows with light off ($S_1=0$) have both
pairs, and all of those rows are unoccupied. Where occupancy varies ($S_1=1$), only $10$ and $11$
occur. In both cases the recoded sources carry the same information about occupancy, so
$\ell_{\{1,2,3\}}=0$ exactly, while $D$ is large: the information moves between atoms.

**Scores on training rows are in-sample.** Of the 445 available score records, 205 are computed on
training rows, 65 on validation rows and 175 on test rows. No record contains a context that is
absent from training, so the uniform fallback for unseen contexts is never exercised.

**Refitting also changes the smoothing.** Each lookup model adds one pseudocount per class to each
context. Merging contexts before fitting gives the merged context one pseudocount per class, where
the original contexts had one each. A comparison of the full model with the refitted model
therefore changes the representation, the counts and the shrinkage together.

**Why refitting worsens the room validation score.** All room validation rows are unoccupied and
have the pair $00$; 196 of the 1,063 rows have light on. In training, the rows with light on and a
merged pair are 570 occupied out of 581, while the rows with light on and the pair $00$ are 668
occupied out of 2,538. Refitting merges the two groups, so the predicted probability of an empty
room for light on and pair $00$ falls from 0.7360 to 0.6026. The loss on each of those 196 rows
rises from 0.3065 to 0.5065, and $(196/1063)\times0.2000=0.0369$. For the 867 rows with light off
the predicted probability changes by less than $10^{-5}$. This accounts for the observed change from
0.0572 to 0.0941 to four decimal places.

**Sparse cells.** The room training distribution occupies 21 of its 32 cells, and six cells hold at
most five rows; the room test distribution occupies 12 cells, two of them single rows; the office
test distribution occupies 7 of 16 cells, one a single row. Atoms computed from such counts are
exact for these finite distributions, but as estimates of a population they are biased. For two
independent fair bits observed twice, the expected empirical mutual information is
$\tfrac14\ln2>0$ although the true value is zero.
