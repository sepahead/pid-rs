# Finite conditional Bayes risk: formal source and local evidence

Author: **Sepehr Mahmoudian**. Published source record, 23 September 2026.

Thirteen frozen classical finite-probability targets passed local Lean compilation, exact-target and whole-module inspection, and fresh kernel replay. A first complete development stage and one final stage used identical candidate source bytes; each had six recorded native calls and root adoption. The [standalone exposition](EXPOSITION.md) derives their mathematical meaning and gives a negative control for supplied-predictor gain. The [descriptive receipt](REPRODUCTION.json) identifies exact public source and raw observation bytes, local execution records and their limits. Portable automated replay and hosted verification of this continuation remain open.

These results extend the separately accepted [four finite-PMF foundations](../PUBLICATION.md) from a single law to conditional prediction under one exact finite law. They establish posterior and null-fiber semantics, supported expected log loss and excess KL, unrestricted Bayes risk, independently defined MI/CMI gains, and an algebraic expected-loss gap for supplied forecasts. They prove no availability-mask or categorical MGW/PID identity, learner improvement, estimator calibration, Rust refinement or physical sensor benefit. The [sensor report](../../../research/embodied-sensor-utility/EXPOSITION.md) retains those separate handwritten and empirical obligations. No scientific novelty is claimed for the classical probability identities.

## Exact source composition

The final source tree has nine Lean modules. Five already published foundation/owner modules below are byte-identical to the locally accepted final stage and remain unchanged. Four new conditional modules were copied exactly from that final stage. Their byte counts and SHA-256 hashes are in the receipt. A replay must compose these files at their module-relative paths with **one version per module name**: the earlier four-target `ImportBaseline`, `ModuleInventory` and `CoreJudge` files under `../sources/FiniteSensorBayesOwner/` are different historical files and cannot replace the conditional owner modules.

## Prospective replay and limits

A future source replay would verify the nine hashes in [REPRODUCTION.json](REPRODUCTION.json), construct a fresh composite source tree with the five shared and four conditional modules, and place only the conditional owner versions on the module search path. It would build `Contract`, `RawTargets`, `FiniteLogScoreTarget` and `Candidate` from the shared public source first. Then it would run the reviewed five-compilation roster at `--trust=0` and a fresh `leanchecker` invocation, retaining exact exits, streams, objects and import observations. That composite-source rebuild has **not** been run by this publication. The accepted final stage reused four earlier accepted foundation objects before its own six fresh calls.

No independent implementation, human or institutional review, formal mask/MGW correspondence, Rust/binary64 evaluator, sampling calibration, trained predictor gain or robotics result follows from local proof checking. The [exposition](EXPOSITION.md) states the exact finite-law assumptions, support restrictions and mathematical derivation. Its prospective dense-table costs are not measured software performance.

The classical logarithmic-score connection is discussed by [Gneiting and Raftery, *Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation*](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437), with the opposite sign convention (their score is maximized). The [foundation publication](../PUBLICATION.md) links the pinned primary Mathlib PMF, integral and logarithm source. The [MGW defining paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03356v5) governs the separate categorical shared-exclusions construction in the sensor report.
