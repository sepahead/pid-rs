Finite-horizon bias bounds for categorical
shared-exclusions atoms
Exact truncation error, population support and limits of use

pid-rs contributors                                                               8 September 2026




A limiting mean theorem does not tell us how long a finite experiment must run before its
expectation is close to its target. This report supplies that missing finite-horizon statement for a
specified categorical shared-exclusions statistic. The five exact Lean propositions were accepted
locally on 8 September 2026. They bound the truncation error of its actual finite-product
expectation. They do not provide a confidence interval around an observed statistic.
The information functional is the finite categorical shared-exclusions PID of Makkeh, Gutknecht
and Wibral (MGW), fixed to arXiv:2002.03356v5, published as Physical Review E 103, 032149.
The prefix experiment and the bounds below are project constructions. All information quantities
use natural logarithms and nats. No scientific novelty, estimator ranking or deployment advantage
is claimed. The theorem-to-proof map identifies the exact formal sources and their separate
evidence boundaries.


1. The data and the question


Let 𝐼 be a finite, nonempty source-index set. Source 𝑖 takes values in a finite alphabet 𝒳𝑖 , and
the target takes values in a finite alphabet 𝒯. A complete row is


                              𝑧 = ((𝑠𝑖 )𝑖∈𝐼 , 𝑡) ∈ 𝒦 = (∏ 𝒳𝑖 ) × 𝒯.
                                                       𝑖∈𝐼

A population probability mass function 𝑝 ∶ 𝒦 → [0, 1] satisfies ∑𝑧 𝑝(𝑧) = 1. The experiment
draws complete rows independently from this same law. Source coordinates and target within a
row may depend arbitrarily. Independence of rows is a model assumption; distinct recorded
values do not establish it.
For an event 𝐴 ⊆ 𝒦, 𝑃 (𝐴) = ∑𝑥∈𝐴 𝑝(𝑥). Write 𝑥𝑖 for source 𝑖’s coordinate and 𝑡𝑥 for the target
coordinate.
For a benign laboratory example, a row might contain fixed categorical outputs from acoustic
and thermal instruments, together with a labeled machine operating mode. A source is the
entire declared instrument output, potentially a tuple of features. The scientific question, source
grouping and target must be fixed before applying the result. A source-pair atom may help
describe a chosen information decomposition; it does not by itself say where to place an instrument
or how to operate a machine.
If the question concerns one source and one target, ordinary mutual information may suffice. If it
concerns prediction or detection, held-out task loss and explicit utility can be more direct. The

A node has an OR between collections and an AND within each

           𝐸𝛼 (𝑧) = ⋃ {𝑥 ∈ 𝒦 ∶ 𝑥𝑖 = 𝑧𝑖 for every 𝑖 ∈ 𝐶},                 𝑉 (𝑧) = {𝑥 ∈ 𝒦 ∶ 𝑡𝑥 = 𝑡𝑧 }.

Plan a sufficient fixed horizon

Population support is different from observed occupancy

The counts 𝐾𝐶 , 𝐾𝐶𝑇 , 𝐾𝑇 refer to positive population fibers. A finite sample may miss possible
categories. Replacing population support with the number of categories observed so far can
shrink the bound without justification. Exhaustive alphabet or structural support bounds can be
valid conservative inputs; empirical estimates of support or a mass floor need their own statistical
guarantees.
An instrument specification that limits one source to 𝑟 categorical states and the target to 𝑠
states supplies 𝐾𝐶𝑇 ≤ 𝑟𝑠, even if some pairs are impossible. Stronger knowledge of possible
pairs can improve this. Recording fewer than 𝑟𝑠 pairs does not, by itself, prove that stronger
population restriction.


Projection can reduce alphabet growth, with limits

For a laboratory system with many categorical sensor outputs, the complete-key alphabet can
grow as a product of alphabet sizes. A member collection 𝐶 can have a much smaller joint
alphabet with the target. Inequality (24) uses that projection to control a query involving the
entire node; it does not require pretending that the other sources were never part of the atom.
This advantage is about categorical support cardinality. It is not a theorem about Euclidean
intrinsic dimension, nearest-neighbor error or immunity to a large source lattice. If every useful
collection has large joint support, or the only justified mass floor is tiny, the bounds may be too
conservative for practical planning. The constant-source example shows that a large bound need
not reflect the actual signed error.


Binning and fitted transformations change what is being estimated

A fixed deterministic transformation applied separately to each iid row preserves the iid structure.
If its outputs are categorical, the theorem concerns the resulting categorical law and its MGW
atom. Binning an analog instrument therefore changes the estimand to the declared binned
variables; refining the bins changes that estimand and may enlarge the support constants.
For a transformation learned from an independent training sample, one may condition on the
realized transformation and examine whether the evaluation experiment and bound inputs satisfy
the fixed-law assumptions. An unconditional bound needs the corresponding conditional bounds
to be integrated or controlled uniformly. This is a model argument, not a newly formalized
training theorem here.
Fitting bins, projections, source groupings or targets on the same evaluation rows can couple
those rows and alter the selected estimand. Separate fold-wise arguments may be possible with
sample splitting or cross-fitting, but these five exports do not supply them automatically. A
shuffled time series is also not thereby established to be iid.

9. Scope, edge cases and proof status


All formulas include ℎ = 0, where the entire logarithmic tail remains. If 𝑞𝑧 = 1, then 𝑎𝑧 = 𝑣𝑧 = 1,
so both tails vanish. If 𝑞𝑧 = 𝑣𝑧 , the negative remainder difference vanishes; if only 𝑣𝑧 = 1, only
the target remainder vanishes automatically. The population-floor theorem permits 𝜂 = 1, which
forces 𝑞𝑧 = 1 at supported anchors. It does not permit 𝜂 = 0. An empty complete-key carrier
cannot support a normalized law; unused alphabet values are allowed and contribute zero mass.
The five accepted declarations refer to finite exact real mathematics under their native probability
model. The semantic checks bound them to both their raw quantified types and their alias
types, and fresh kernel runs checked the selected dependency closure with the permitted axioms
Classical.choice, Quot.sound and propext. Repeated and cosmetic runs add no new theorem
families. The detailed proof map separates formal evidence, source interpretation, development
failures and publication status.
The result supplies no finite unbiasedness, variance, MSE, confidence coverage, stopping-law,
regeneration, Rust/Python refinement, cross-platform numerical equality or application quali-
fication theorem. It does not transfer to Ehrlich’s continuous shared exclusions, another PID
functional or a hyperbolic estimator. Finite categorical equality events, exact finite sums and a
declared iid row law are the ingredients of this proof; a distance metric or embedding does not
replace them.
For sensor-data analysis, these bounds can inform an offline investigation of a fixed categorical
information question. They do not establish placement, fusion, alerting or real-time superiority
over MI/CMI, task loss, ablations or explicit utility. Those comparisons require separate data
and decision criteria.
