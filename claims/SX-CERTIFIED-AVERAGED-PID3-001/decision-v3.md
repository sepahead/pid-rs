## Source correspondence and the Equation (4)/Equation (6) distinction

The bridge records exact SHA-256 and byte counts for arXiv `2002.03356v5`, its source archive, and
the extracted `apstemplate.tex` member. It maps ten semantic anchors to PDF pages, equations, and
source-line intervals. Hashes identify bytes; they do not authenticate authorship, priority, or the
meaning assigned to those bytes.

A fresh owner-controlled HTTPS acquisition replay on 2026-09-03 UTC reproduced all three byte
counts and hashes and verified that the archive contained exactly one top-level
`apstemplate.tex`. The replay commands and outcome are bound in the source map and machine record.
This improves reproducibility but is not independent custody, authenticated provenance, or trusted
time.

Each of the ten anchor records separately states the paper location and meaning, its repository
analogue, assumptions preserved unchanged, declared representation or unit changes, an inference
that is forbidden, and the evidence required for a stronger claim. In particular, source masks
and stable hexadecimal keys are representations of finite source collections, not new mathematical
objects; multiplication by $\ln2$ changes units but not zero/sign/linear identities; and neither
representation change proves parser, formal, or compiled-code refinement.

The central disambiguation is:

- MGW Equation (4), for a subset of *individual source realizations*, joins the corresponding
  equality statements by OR. In local antichain notation this is represented by singleton
  collections such as `01+02`.
- MGW Equations (5)--(8), for a collection of source collections, use AND inside each collection
  and OR across collections. One joint collection such as `03` therefore means
  $(S_1=s_1)\land(S_2=s_2)$.

These objects are not interchangeable. On source-equality pattern `010`, `01+02` is true while
`03` is false. This one-row witness rejects both an AND-across-branches mutant and an OR-inside-a-
joint-collection mutant. It also explains why a superficially plausible reuse of Equation (4) at
every Equation (6) collection changes the mathematical object.

For a supported keyed row $z=(s_1,s_2,s_3,t)$, let $E_\alpha(z)$ denote the resulting OR-of-AND
source event. Given arbitrary nonnegative finite row counts $c_x$ with $c_z>0$, define

$$
N=\sum_xc_x,\quad
U_{\alpha,z}=\sum_xc_x\mathbf1\{x\in E_\alpha(z)\},\quad
T_z=\sum_xc_x\mathbf1\{t_x=t\},
$$

$$
V_{\alpha,z}=\sum_xc_x\mathbf1\{x\in E_\alpha(z),\ t_x=t\}.
$$

The keyed row belongs to every branch event selected by its own values and has the keyed target.
Moreover,

$$
\mathbf1\{E_\alpha\cap(T=t)\}\le\mathbf1\{E_\alpha\},
\qquad
\mathbf1\{E_\alpha\cap(T=t)\}\le\mathbf1\{T=t\}.
$$

Multiplication by $c_x\ge0$ and summation give

$$
0<c_z\le V_{\alpha,z}\le U_{\alpha,z}\le N,
\qquad
V_{\alpha,z}\le T_z\le N.
$$

This is an arbitrary-finite-row indicator/count argument. It does not prove that arbitrary bytes
decode uniquely to those rows; D1 remains open.

## Concrete finite reconstruction

The production checker starts with the seven nonzero source-subset masks. It enumerates all
$2^7-1=127$ nonempty mask families and retains exactly those in which no two masks are comparable.
It therefore derives, rather than assumes, the complete 18-node carrier.

For antichains $\alpha$ and $\beta$, it implements the MGW Appendix order

$$
\alpha\preceq\beta
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall b\in\beta\;\exists a\in\alpha:\;a\subseteq b.
$$

With cumulative nodes indexing rows and atom nodes indexing columns, it builds
$Z_{ij}=\mathbf1\{\alpha_j\preceq\alpha_i\}$. It obtains 129 true entries among the 324 ordered
pairs. Exact `Fraction` Gaussian elimination constructs the integer matrix $M$ and requires both
$MZ=I$ and $ZM=I$; $M$ has 65 nonzero entries.

The same checker derives:

| Object | Exact count |
|---|---:|
| Source-event truth cases | $18\times8=144$ |
| Event/target-intersection truth cases | $18\times8\times2=288$ |
| Source-label permutations | $3!=6$ |

Each permutation must preserve the carrier, all order relations, and event truth when source labels
and equality coordinates are relabeled together. This is source-label equivariance. It is not the
same statement as reordering collections within one antichain, and it does not by itself establish
an MGW axiom.
